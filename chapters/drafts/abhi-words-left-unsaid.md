# abhi- words left unsaid.

*POV: Abhi | Timeline: year 2*

---

Amy had moved to Bangalore a day before. She had been struggling with her breakup for over 2 months now. She thought the change of place might help her. It broke all our hearts to lose Amy like that. We had been waiting all evening for her to reach her new house from office so we could talk to her.
Ash was pacing the living room while Leo sat quietly on the sofa. I sat on our deck watching the traffic on the road to distract myself from the sende of loss i was feeling. The moment all 3 of our mobiles alerted a msg we knew it was from Amy. She was finally home. 
'Abhi, call her , call her' said Leo and he sat next to me. Ash sat on the other side. 
' Hi' she said cheerfully.
' hey , how are you!' Ash said.
' how was your day?' I asked.
' how is the new office? ' Leo added. 
' It is nice. It has a gym and all' she said. ' I kept getting lost all day while getting coffee or going to the restroom' she laughed.
' you will get used to it', said Ash.
'yeah,' she said.' did you guys have dinner? What did you order today?' Friday was our 'order out' day.
' we got pizza from the little shop downstairs ' Leo answered.
' aww, I already miss it' ,she said trying to hide that she was really missing having dinner with us.
' what are you having for dinner.' Ash asked.
That took her by surprise.' I.. hmm.. I did not even think about it' she said slapping her forehead. ' just slipped my mind..  I will look for something around.' most of the times she is alone for dinner she would just skip it. 
' she is going to skip dinner', said Leo.
' let me order something for her.' Ash said looking at his mobile and ignoring Amy's protest of  ' hey I won't skip dinner. ' and ' you don't have to, guys'
' Look ! they have a place that serves kid's size meals near your house.' Ash laughed.' And you can add milk as well!'
' haha, very funny' said Amy said sarcastically.
' done' said Ash. Amy made a face.
' thanks! ' she said finally. ' I don't know how I am going to manage without you.' her eyes glistening.
' you will be just fine.' Leo said quickly. We all did not know why to say next. 
' we aren't going anywhere, we are here', I said. 
Amy smiled , ' did you really order milk, Ash. I could use it for tea. ' she laughed.
' I ordered chocolate milk' he said.
' even better !..talk to you guys later. '
Leo went in and sat on the sofa with his head back staring at the ceiling. 
' he isn't taking it very well' I said  to Ash.
' let me handle him.' Ash replied.
Ash went in and sat next to him. It wasn't just Amy who was affected by her break up. We were all devastated too. 
I called up Tanu. I needed to hear her voice to clam this sinking feeling in my heart.
' hi ' she said.
' Hi' I answered.
' what's wrong, Abhi.' she could tell my mood from my voice. She always could. 
' just spoke to Amy' I sighed
' hmm. Is she doing ok?' tanu asked.
' no, ' I said desperately,' Tanu,it is so hard to know she is struggling and to not be able to do anything. I feel so helpless.'  
I sat there for long even after I finished talking to Tanu. Leo and Ash had gone to their rooms to sleep. I watched the traffic on the road below lessen. It was almost midnight.
She was so far.. it was like this huge chasm was between us and I couldn't reach her.

'Amy..' I typed on my phone and saw her typing back..
' Abhi..:)' she replied and I called her.
' why are you still up.' I asked 
' new place , couldn't sleep.' she sniffed. She had been crying. I could sense it in her voice. I wanted to tell her to come back. That she was lonely there and I was lonelier. 
' All your boxes reached there? When do you start unpacking?' I asked instead.
' Tomorrow I hope . At least will get the kitchen set up tomorrow ' she said. Come back, Amy,! I wanted to plead. I wanted to hug her tight. 
' how is the weather there?' I asked stupidly.
' pretty good. You don't even need an AC! ' she said. 
I miss you, you idiot,I thought.
' you should sleep now', I said instead.
' yeah, you too.'
I had never been good at expressing what I was feeling. And Amy had never known how to read between lines. The chasm just got wider.
