# Ash - The  Night Watch

*POV: Ash | Timeline: year 1*

---

The funeral had been in the afternoon.

Leo had spoken to people for three hours straight — aunts who needed to be seated, cousins who needed to be fed, the priest who needed someone to confirm the order of things. He had been everywhere and said the right words and refilled water glasses and accepted hugs from people whose names he was cycling through on instinct. He had not stopped moving once.

Ash had watched him do it. That was mostly what Ash was good for at funerals — watching, keeping out of the way, being a body that could carry things when someone needed a hand free.

Amy had stayed close to Leo all day without being in his way. That was harder to do than it looked, and she made it look like nothing. When an aunt had needed someone to sit with her, Amy had sat. When the water ran out, Amy had quietly refilled it before Leo noticed it needed doing. When someone had said something clumsy and well-meaning about grief, Amy had smoothly redirected the conversation so Leo didn't have to. She hadn't done any of it for credit. She'd done it so Leo could keep moving without having to think.

Ash had watched her do that for three hours and hadn't said anything about it.

Abhi had handled the parts that required someone with a face that didn't give things away.

On the way home nobody spoke much. Leo sat in the front and looked out the window at nothing in particular. Amy was in the back between Ash and Abhi. Ash noticed she kept her eyes on the back of Leo's head the whole ride — not watching him, exactly. More like keeping him company from a distance. Making sure he knew, without saying so, that someone was paying attention.

When they got home Leo said *I'm going to lie down* and went to his room and shut the door.

Not a slam. Just — shut.

The three of them stood in the living room for a moment.

"Should I—" Amy started.

"Give him some time," Abhi said quietly.

She nodded. She didn't look convinced, but she nodded.

Ash saw her glance at the closed door once before she went to the kitchen. He heard her moving around in there for a while. The sounds of something being cooked, quietly, without announcement.

---

Ash woke up at quarter past two for a glass of water.

He saw the television first — low volume, some nature documentary, the blue light of it spilling into the hallway. Then he saw Amy, curled into the corner of the sofa with her knees pulled up, still in the clothes she'd worn to the funeral. The BB-8 mug was on the side table, long cold. And on the kitchen counter — Ash clocked it immediately — a plate, covered carefully with another plate on top to keep the heat in. She'd made food for Leo and left it waiting.

He stood in the doorway for a moment.

"You're awake," he said.

"I'm awake," she confirmed.

"It's two in the morning."

"I know what time it is, Ash."

He came and sat at the other end of the sofa. On screen something was happening with a migration pattern. He watched it for a moment, then looked at her. She was still watching the door to Leo's room. Not the television.

"He hasn't eaten," she said. Not an accusation. Just a fact she'd been sitting with for hours.

"I know."

"He didn't eat at the funeral either. I checked." A pause. "I kept trying to get food near him and he kept moving."

Ash didn't say anything.

"I just—" She stopped. Pulled the edge of her dupatta over her hands, the fidgeting thing she did when she couldn't find words fast enough. "He was so busy all day. Taking care of everyone. And now he's in there alone and I don't—" She exhaled. "I don't want him to wake up and it be three in the morning and everyone's asleep and he has to just — sit in it. By himself."

She said it plainly, like it was obvious. Like of course you didn't let someone you loved sit alone in grief at three in the morning. Like there was no other option she'd seriously considered.

"Okay," Ash said.

"You don't have to stay."

"I know."

He reached over and stole half the blanket. She let him. They watched the migration in silence.

---

An hour passed. Maybe more. Ash had half-drifted into something like sleep, his head tipped against the sofa cushion, when he heard it — the soft mechanical sound of a door handle turning.

Amy heard it before he did. She was already sitting up, already alert, by the time Leo appeared in the hallway.

He looked the way you look at three in the morning when you've been lying in the dark for hours and none of it has helped. Still in his funeral clothes. Eyes that had been crying and then stopped and then probably started again. He blinked at the television, at Amy, at Ash — like he'd expected the flat to be empty and wasn't sure what to do with it not being.

"Hey," Amy said. Soft. Like she'd been waiting, which she had.

Leo stood there for a moment.

"I'm hungry," he said. Uncertain. Almost apologetic, like he wasn't sure he was allowed to need something small and ordinary in the middle of grief.

Amy was already up.

No fuss, no commentary — she just went to the kitchen and took the covered plate off the counter and put it in the microwave. Like she'd known. Like she'd been counting on this exact moment arriving and had made sure she'd be ready for it.

Ash stood and stretched and steered Leo gently toward the sofa.

A door opened down the hall. Abhi appeared, reading the room in one look, and came and sat at the coffee table without turning a light on. Folded himself onto the floor, back against it.

"Hi," he said.

"Hi," Leo said.

Amy came back with the plate and set it on the table in front of Leo. Then she sat down beside him — close, not performing it, just there. The way she'd been there all day. The way she'd apparently been planning to be there all night if that's what it took.

Leo looked at the food. Then at her.

"Are you okay?" she asked. Sad, quiet. Not trying to make it easier than it was.

Leo shook his head.

His eyes filled. He pressed his mouth together, and for a moment Ash thought he might hold it — and then Leo said *no* and it broke on the way out, and Amy's arms were around him before the word had finished landing. Not tentative about it. Both arms, pulling him in completely, her hand at the back of his head.

Leo's shoulders shook. He made a sound Ash had never heard from him.

Amy held on. She didn't say *it's okay* because she wasn't going to lie to him. She didn't say anything. She just kept holding on, her chin against the top of his head, her eyes closed.

Ash put a hand on Leo's back.

Abhi reached up from the floor and wrapped his hand around Leo's.

The food went cold again. Outside, the city kept going, indifferent. In here the four of them stayed exactly where they were, in the blue light of the television, until Leo's breathing came back to him.

Amy didn't let go until he did.

Then she quietly got up and reheated the plate.
