# Miles -  always shows up

*POV: Miles | Timeline: year 2*

---

I knew he was coming before he knocked.

My father had called that morning, too carefully casual, asking how the course was going and at the end of it he'd said *I may have mentioned to Nick that you were in London. *

So when the knock came, I opened the door before he could knock a second time.

Nick looked at me the way he'd been looking at me since we were children and I'd done something he considered inadvisable. Like he was checking for damage and compiling a list simultaneously.

I let him. It was Nick. Arguing with that expression was a waste of both our time.

"So," he said.

"So," I said.

He stepped inside without being invited, which was also Nick. He looked at the apartment — one window, desk with books stacked sideways, the kettle — and then he looked back at me.

"You broke off an engagement and moved to London and the way I found out was through your father, who I had to call because you weren't picking up."

"I didn't move to London. I'm doing a course. There's a difference."

"Miles. What the hell happened?"

"I handled it."

"That's not what I'm asking. Why is it that you *handling it* and *telling me* have to be separate things?"

I looked out the window. 

"He wanted to know where I was all the time," I said. "Who I was with. How long." I kept it level. It was easier that way. "He had opinions about certain friendships."

Nick didn't interrupt. his jaw tightened.

"And then?"

---

"We argued."

His eyes stayed on mine. I looked away first.

"He raised his hand."

Nick went very still.

For a moment he didn't say anything. He didn't move. He just stood there in the middle of my apartment without blinking, without breathing. 

"Did he hit you." his voice trembled. there was panic in his eyes.

It wasn't a question.

"No, " I said and a small breath escaped him. "  I know karate," I said. "And kickboxing. He was on the floor before he could actually touch me."

He reached out and took my arm. Struggling to speak. 

"Nick—"

"Just." He stopped. "Give me a second."

I let him have it.

He let go of my arm and sat down. Like his legs could not handle the weight of what he heard. He put his elbows on his knees and looked at the floor for a moment. 

He looked up.

"You were alone," he said. It wasn't an accusation. It was something worse than an accusation — it was just the fact of it, landing on him in real time. "You were alone in that room and I didn't—" He stopped again.

"I handled it," I repeated.

"I know you handled it." His voice was quiet. "Miles, I know. That's not—" He exhaled. "You could have been hurt. In a room I didn't know you were in, in a situation I didn't know was happening, and there is nothing I could have—"

He didn't finish the sentence.

He didn't need to. I understood what he was saying. Not that I had failed to tell him but the shape of a world where something happens to someone you care about and you are simply elsewhere, unknowing, unreachable-

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"I didn't want anyone worrying." I said. "It's done. There's nothing anyone could have done after the fact except—" I stopped.

"Except what?"

"Except exactly this." I gestured, vaguely, at the room. At him sitting on my sofa having gotten on a plane. "Look at you. Look at your face right now."

"My face is fine."

"Your face is not fine, Nick." I did not want everyone doing what he was doing right now. Looking at me like a victim. It was much easier that they though I was hot headed and broke off my engagement because of some trivial argument. 

" Am I supposed to be fine?! Of  course I not fine!  What else did you want me to do? I just found out that some one I love has been dealing with all this all on her own."

I blinked. Someone I love. Something about hearing it now made my chest tighten unexpectedly.

"I didn't want you to pity me," I said.

The word landed and I didn't flinch but I felt it.

"Is that what you think?" Something shifted in him.  "You think I got on a flight because I was going to come here and feel *sorry* for you? That's how little you think of me?"

"It's not about you—"

"It is a little bit about me, actually, because you made a decision about what I would feel before you gave me the chance to feel anything." He leaned forward slightly. "No one is looking at you and seeing a victim. The only person in this room with that picture in their head is you."

"I don't want to be looked at like something that needs managing," 

"Miles." he said exasperated. "I'm not here to fix you or manage you. I came here thinking you are making the biggest mistake of your life calling off your wedding " he expression softened,  "but if that was what you wanted I came here to stand by you anyway."

 He moved closer to me - "Miles...  Never shut me out like that please."

And then I told him what really hurt me about this whole situation " I am not worried about Raj hurting me , Nick , But it was the moment before he raised his arm, and i realized how very wrong i was about him. I trusted him . How could I be so wrong." Nick put his arm around me.Without really thinking about it, I leaned into the touch.It felt good.Not having to carry it alone.

"I am *so* done with dating," I said to lighten the mood. "I should just marry you and be done with it."

I meant it as a joke. But it did not seem to land quiet well.

Nick didn't say anything for a moment. his grip on my arm grew a bit harder. For one suspended second he looked like he was about to say something. Instead, he looked away.  "You dont have to marry me or anyone just to be safe " he said as he let me go. Then he exhaled and shook his head, "This is too much. I am hungry and tired. Just order something while I take a nap."  

He laid on the sofa covering his eyes with his elbows.. I picked up my phone and ordered dinner. 

I looked at him Shoes still on. One arm over his eyes and completely exhausted, but still here by my side, no matter where in the world I was. I thought about the fact that he was the only person I knew who could be angry at me and worried about me at exactly the same time, who could call me out and show up, who had known me long enough to know every version of my pride and had gotten on a plane anyway.

Nick had always been there. Always. And for the first time, I wondered what it meant that he was the person I looked for whenever everything else fell apart. And suddenly that fact felt less ordinary than it ever had before.
