# POV GUIDE — FORGET ME NOT
*Cross-reference: chapter-pov-map.md*

---

## STRUCTURE

The novel uses multiple close third-person POVs. Each chapter is assigned to one narrator. POV does not shift within a chapter.

The present-day thread and the flashback thread can use different narrators for the same period — what Leo experienced in Year 3 and what Amy experienced are separate chapters, not blended.

---

## THE NARRATORS

### AMY
**When to use:** Her emotional arc toward Leo (shown through action, not declaration). The amnesia recovery — her experience of not knowing. Scenes where her optimism is the structural load-bearer.

**What her POV gives the reader:** The cost of the choice to be okay. The love that is visible in behaviour before she names it. The specific texture of not knowing what you've lost.

**Constraint:** Amy's chapters cannot show the reader what she doesn't know in the amnesia arc. The reader experiences her gaps with her.

---

### LEO
**When to use:** The present-day amnesia thread — his position as a stranger in his own house. Key flashbacks: the Bengaluru trip, the proposal, the monsoon night. Scenes where his love is demonstrated through action before language.

**What his POV gives the reader:** The full weight of what Amy doesn't remember. The restraint of someone who has everything to say and almost no context in which to say it.

**Constraint:** Leo is not the most perceptive person in any room. He acts before he understands. Do not write him as emotionally articulate — write him as emotionally committed.

---

### NICK
**When to use:** His vigil at Amy's hospital bedside. Scenes before the fake death. His relationship with Miles. Scenes where the gap between performance and feeling is the subject.

**What his POV gives the reader:** The friendship from inside. The specific texture of knowing someone completely and watching them not know you.

**Constraint:** Nick tells only Miles about the fake death. His POV chapters before his return must not reveal this to the reader before it's time.

---

### ABHI
**When to use:** The gift-collecting scenes. The airport goodbye. London scenes. The Tanu relationship (when written). Scenes where precision and restraint are the emotional register.

**What his POV gives the reader:** The cost of the right choice made badly. The specific love that expresses itself in noticing — fairy lights, a bedroom facing east, the impossibly soft teddy bear.

**Constraint:** Abhi's feelings for Amy are resolved — he chose Tanu and means it. His chapters must not reopen this as longing. The complexity lives in having made the right choice imperfectly.

---

## POV ASSIGNMENT PRINCIPLES

- **Present-day scenes:** Primarily Leo and Nick. Amy's POV enters as she begins to recover.
- **Early flashbacks (Year 1–2):** Leo and Amy.
- **Bengaluru period:** Amy primarily, Leo for the trip.
- **Nick's introduction:** Amy's POV — she is the one discovering him.
- **The fake death arc:** Nick (before) and Amy (the call to Miles). Leo (the floor scene).
- **The London period:** Abhi. Tanu when her scenes are written.
- **The wedding:** Multiple POVs across chapters.

---

## WHAT TO AVOID

- POV drift within a chapter
- Giving a character knowledge they don't have in their own POV
- Writing Leo as emotionally articulate rather than emotionally committed
- Writing Nick's performance as cynical rather than armoured
- Writing Abhi's restraint as coldness rather than precision
- Writing Amy's optimism as naivety rather than choice
