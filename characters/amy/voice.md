# AMY — VOICE GUIDE
*Cross-reference: profile.md*

---

## HOW AMY SOUNDS

Amy's internal narration is **observational, not declarative**. She notices. She registers. She does not announce what she feels.

Her voice is warm, quick, specific. She notices small things — the wrong chair, the tea going cold, whether someone has eaten. She moves toward people. Her language is tactile.

She does not perform optimism. It reads as fact in her narration because she has decided it is fact. The cost of that decision lives in the gap between what she narrates and what the reader can see.

---

## SPEECH PATTERNS
- Talks fast. Follows tangents. Returns to the point eventually.
- Says the real thing plainly, once, then leaves it alone if it's not picked up.
- Does not press. Does not repeat herself to make a point land.
- Physically expressive while talking — touches arms, reaches for hands, turns to face people fully.
- Cannot lie. Does not try to conceal things well because her face defeats the attempt.

---

## INTERNAL NARRATION
- Short to medium sentences. Rhythm is quick but not breathless.
- Notices specifics — objects, temperatures, sounds, other people's posture.
- Does not over-explain her own emotions. Names them briefly if at all, then moves to action.
- Humour is present — dry when she's under pressure, warm when she's comfortable.
- **Optimism in narration reads as decision, not feeling.** It's an act of will that happens below the surface of the sentence.

---

## WHAT SHE NEVER DOES
- Asks for help before she's past the point where asking feels possible
- Centres herself in a scene if someone else is struggling
- Lets people see the cost of her optimism
- Uses her pain as leverage

---

## SAMPLE LINES

> She made tea for everyone. Leo made tea for her. She noticed this later, much later, in the way you notice things that were already over by the time you understood them.

> He was going to be fine. She had decided this. It was not a feeling, exactly — more like a position she had taken and intended to hold.

> *There's no proof,* she said, because that was true. She needed it to stay true for a little longer.

> The sticky note was still in her wallet. She'd meant to throw it away several times. She hadn't.

> Cooking felt like the only reasonable thing to do. People still needed to eat. The world hadn't stopped requiring that.

---

## DISTINGUISHING AMY FROM OTHER POVs
- **vs. Leo:** Leo acts before he understands. Amy understands and then chooses. Both move toward people — the motivation is different.
- **vs. Nick:** Nick performs; Amy means. Both say real things sideways. Nick's humour is armour; Amy's is reflex.
- **vs. Abhi:** Abhi observes from stillness; Amy observes in motion. Abhi's narration has more negative space. Amy fills space — with words, warmth, presence.
