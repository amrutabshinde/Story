# leo- knife in the dark

*POV: Leo | Timeline: year 3*

---

It was 2 a.m. I was alone in the house, trying to sleep. Amy hadn’t replied to my messages since evening. Then my phone lit up with Nick’s name.

I answered, my stomach twisting.  
“Hello?”  

“Leo,” Nick said, his voice urgent and shaky. “We were attacked at my place.”  

“What?!”  

He was sobbing. “There was a robber. He came at us with a knife.” He took a ragged breath. “Amy got stabbed in the leg. I drove her to the hospital. She’s in surgery right now.” His voice cracked. “Leo… I let her get hurt. The doctors say she’ll be fine, but I let her get hurt.”  

“I’m coming,” I said, forcing the words out through the panic threatening to freeze me. “I’m on my way.”  

“Nick,” I added quickly, “how are you?” I didn’t care as much about him, but I knew she did.  

“I… I have a cut on my arm, but I’m fine.”  

Only later did I learn the full truth: he had a deep gash on his arm, not as severe as Amy’s but bad enough that he lost a lot of blood. He had refused to let anyone treat him until Amy was stable. “Not me,” he’d told them. “Her. Fix her first.” He had called me before finally allowing the doctors to look at him, then fainted from blood loss right after the call.

I caught the first flight to Bengaluru. It was the longest journey of my life. I kept praying she would be okay.

I reached the hospital early the next morning. Amy was out of surgery and in the post-op room, still unconscious. They wouldn’t let me in. The surgery had gone without complications. Nick was being discharged, his left arm stitched and in a sling. He was awake, so I went to see him first.

I knew it wasn’t his fault. Still, the question gnawed at me: *Why Amy? Why not him?*

“Hi,” I said as he walked out of the room, hand cradled against his chest.  

“Hi.” His voice was tired. “Amy still not awake?”  

“Not yet. How are you?”  

“I’m okay.” Then, almost as an afterthought, he added, “I wish we could switch places.”  

I assumed he meant with Amy.  

“I wish so too,” I replied.  

He gave a small, bitter laugh. “But Amy wouldn’t have it any other way.”  

He fell silent. The weight of the night was clearly crushing him.

A few hours later, Amy was moved to a private room. We were finally allowed to see her. She was hooked up to IVs and monitors, a bruise darkening her forehead. I had come so close to losing her. Nick walked in behind me. He quietly adjusted the blanket over her feet, then sank into a chair. I sat beside her, holding her hand.

“I let her get hurt,” Nick whispered, tears slipping down his face. “I’m so sorry, Leo.”  

“Nick, I’m sure you did everything you could,” I said. I believed it. Nick and Amy shared a bond that was almost otherworldly, like two halves of one soul. Sometimes I envied how close they were.  

“She jumped in front of me,” he sobbed. “I should have protected her.”  

Just then, Amy stirred. We both stood up. Her eyes stayed closed, but her face twitched. It was painful to see her so still and quiet.

I eventually dozed off on the sofa. Nick stayed in the chair. When Amy finally woke, it was Nick who noticed first and gently shook me awake.

She opened her eyes, blinking against the dim lights. When she saw us, a weak smile touched her lips.  

“Hi…”  

“Hi,” I rushed to her side and took her hand. She squeezed back. Nick stood at the foot of the bed.  

“Amy, how are you?” I asked.  

She made a small face, as if trying to remember. Then realization hit. Horror flooded her expression.  

“Nick!” she said sharply. “He had a knife! How’s your arm?”  

Nick laughed dryly. “Amy, how are *you*?”  

She ignored him, struggling to sit up. “How’s your arm? Are you hurt badly? Why is your hand in a sling?”  

Nick closed his eyes, clearly asking for patience. He must have realized arguing with her was pointless.  

“I’m fine,” he said.  

“He got a few stitches,” I added. “They discharged him this morning. He just needs to rest his arm so the stitches heal properly.”  

“How are *you* feeling?” Nick asked her.  

“I’m feeling good. Nothing’s hurting,” she replied, a little too quickly. Nick shot me a knowing look.  

“I’ll give you two a minute,” he said and stepped out.

I kissed her hand. “Ever since you came into my life, I swear I’ve had a mini heart attack every few weeks.”  

She smiled apologetically.  

“Leo… it’s hurting a lot,” she whispered once she was sure Nick was gone.  

I felt my chest tighten. She’d been hiding the pain, not wanting to worry him. “Let me call the doctor.”  

“Leo… am I going to be able to walk?” The question had clearly been weighing on her since she woke up.  

“Of course,” I said, trying to sound confident. “It was just a cut. They’re going to discharge you in a day or two.” I lied, but it worked.  

“Really?” Her face brightened. “If the doctors are discharging me, it can’t be that bad.”  

“Yeah. You might have a slight limp, but don’t worry—I’ll carry you everywhere for the rest of our lives,” I joked, leaning down to kiss her forehead.

When I stepped out, Nick was waiting right by the door. He raised his eyebrows. “So?”  

“It’s hurting, but she’s alright.”  

The doctor was already on his way—Nick had called him.

A few minutes later, the nurse added more painkillers to Amy’s IV while Nick and I stood at the end of her bed. Amy stared at the drip as if she could will it to work faster.  

“It’ll take a little time to kick in,” Nick said softly. He had been watching her the whole time.  

Amy looked at him sheepishly, realizing he already knew.  

“No more lies,” he told her.  

“No more guilty crying then,” she shot back.  

“Fair enough.”  

Idiots. Complete idiots.
