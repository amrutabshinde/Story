The thing about Abhi's Xbox is that it makes you stupid.

Not in a bad way. In a specific way where an hour disappears and you're not entirely sure how, and the only thing that exists is whatever is happening on screen and whether Ash is cheating, which he always is, and whether Abhi will admit it, which he never does. It's a very effective system.

We'd been at it since I got home from office. Shoes still on for the first twenty minutes — I hadn't even changed. Ash had commandeered the good controller, which was the cheating, and Abhi was on the floor with his back against the sofa, and I was next to him, and the volume was — the volume was fairly high, I'll admit that.

The doorbell rang.

We all looked at each other.

"That'll be the food," Ash said.

"I didn't order food," Abhi said.

"I ordered food," Ash said. "Forty minutes ago. While you were busy losing."

"I wasn't—"

I was already getting up. My phone was somewhere on the sofa cushion, I registered it vaguely as I stood — the screen was lit up, several notifications, which I didn't look at. I'd deal with it after food.

I opened the door.

Amy was on the other side of it.

She'd been crying. Not recently-stopped crying — actively, right now, tears still coming, eyes red, mascara in two dark lines down her face. She was still in her work clothes. Her breathing was wrong, the kind of wrong that happens when you've been crying so hard your body forgets the rhythm of it.

I took a step back.

"Why aren't you answering your phone?" she said. It came out loud, higher than her normal register.

"We were play—"

"I have been calling you for the past one hour." Her voice broke on the last word. "One hour, Leo."

"Amy, what's wrong?" Abhi had appeared behind me.

"There was an accident." She was trying to get the words out in order and her breathing wasn't cooperating. "Near your office. A tanker. It caught fire. People are — a lot of people are hurt, Leo. I saw it on the news and I tried to call and you weren't — and I kept calling and you didn't — I thought—"

Her face did something complicated.

"I thought — Leo—"

She sat down. Not gracefully, not deliberately — her knees just went and she sat down on the floor of the doorway, and then she was really crying, the kind of crying that takes the whole body, shoulders shaking, one hand pressed over her mouth like she could hold some of it back.

I knelt down next to her. I wasn't sure what I was doing exactly. My brain was still a few steps behind the situation.

"Amy," I said. "I'm alright."

It came out sounding a bit useless. She kept saying *I was so worried, Leo* between sobs, not quite hearing me yet, and I pulled her in because that seemed like the thing to do, and she came without resistance — forehead against my chest, her hands finding the front of my shirt, warm even through the fabric.

"I'm alright," I said again. "Amy. I'm right here."

It felt almost presumptuous to keep saying it. *I'm alright* — as if that fixed the one hour she'd spent not knowing. But it was the only true thing I had.

Her breathing started to slow, in increments. I could feel it happening — the shaking going out of her by degrees, her grip on my shirt loosening slightly.

"I'm sorry," I said. "I didn't see my phone. I'm sorry."

She didn't say anything. Just exhaled, long and unsteady, against my chest.

Then something shifted — I felt it before I understood it. She went still in a different way. Like she'd suddenly become aware of where she was. She stepped back quickly, out of my arms, and her eyes went immediately to the floor. Ash was already there with a glass of water, appearing from nowhere the way Ash does. Abhi's arm went around her shoulders. She took the glass and stared at it with tremendous focus, as if it contained something she needed to study.

She wasn't going to look at me. I don't know why I noticed that.

"You got snot on my shirt," I said.

She laughed. It came out wrong because she was mid-sip, and she coughed and choked and Ash thumped her on the back, and Abhi said *thank you Leo, very helpful*, and just like that the terrible feeling in the room broke open into something survivable.

---

I went to my room to change.

That was what I told myself. And it was true — the shirt needed changing. But I stood in the middle of my room for a while after I'd changed it, doing nothing in particular.

The thing was — and I'm a little embarrassed to put it plainly, even to myself — it felt like something. To have her fret over me like that. To have her call for an hour, to have her face do what it did at the door, to have her sitting on the floor of my doorway because her knees stopped working over the idea that I might be hurt.

*I thought, Leo.* She hadn't finished the sentence. I kept finishing it different ways and none of them made me feel less like I needed to sit down.

I'd been on her sofa when she had a nightmare once and I'd seen her cry at a film and I'd watched her break up with Vic at a distance I wasn't supposed to be standing at. I knew she felt things. That wasn't new information.

But she'd called for an hour.

I changed my shirt and went back out to the living room. Ash had restarted the game. Abhi was on the sofa now next to Amy, who had the GoT fleece around her shoulders even though it wasn't cold and was resolutely pretending to be very interested in what was happening on screen.

I sat down on the other side of her.

After a moment she leaned sideways, very slightly, and her shoulder was against my arm. She didn't comment on it. I didn't either.

I picked up my controller.

The game resumed
