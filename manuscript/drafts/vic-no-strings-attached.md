# Vicky - No strings attached

*POV: Vic | Timeline: year 2*

---

I walked into the bar and saw Abhi at the bar. A quick look around the bar told me Amy wasn't there with him. I wasn't sure if I was relieved or disappointed. I took a deep breath as I walked toward Abhi.
'hi'
He grunted. He has always hated me. Ever since I started dating his best friend he has hated me. We usually met with Amy around and she intervenes when things start getting ugly. I wondered what he was going to be me for this time. Things were going great with Amy, more that great actually. So much that I even agreed to meet this bulldog of a friend of hers.
' so you are going to the trek to mountains with Amy'
Ah the trek, yeah, that is what he wants to scold me about. Going to a trek  to lower Himalayas has been on Amy's wish list for ever. You have to live in tents and sleep in sleeping bags and climb for miles.
Of Course Abhi has been stopping his precious Amy from going there.
'She has been dreaming to that dangerous trek for almost 5 years now, and I had been successful in changing her mind every summer. And u show up and actually enroll in it with her' said Abhi.
'Dude ,cmon,  it isn't like i wanted to go. But if she does isn't it better I go with. '
' I agree , it is nice of you to do that.'
'eh' I mumbled ' thanks?'
' I mean it , I really think it means a lot to her that you are going with her'
I was surprised and a bit scared, was he really praising me or was it a joke.
'i guess, m happy she is happy' I said , not really knowing what to say.
' but why are you doing this for her' abhi said , in a tone that I was much familiar with. 
' c'mon dude, she is my girlfriend after all' I said.
' but you guys were not going to get serious,' he said ,' like wasn't this arrangement going to be just temporary?'
' it is temporary, not like we plan to get married or something' not sure what he was getting at. Amy and I had an understanding, we knew we would never work out forever. She wanted to settle down with a big home and a backyard and curtains and stuff. And I wanted to travel the world. But we enjoyed each other's company and it was good to have a casual relationship with no strings attached. 
' hmm' he said 
' what do u want to say abhi', he was annoying me now.
' I think you are in love with her' he said. 
' are you high! ' I asked annoyed. ' you of all ppl should know what Amy and I are ' 
'exactly' he said calmly.
' just a few months back you were biting my head off for not being in a serious relationship with Amy.' 
' I never wanted you to have a serious relationship with her. But whether you accept it or not you are in one now. '
' cut the crap , Abhi' I felt my temper rise.
' if this arrangement of your was going to be temporary why are you still together' he said a bit more loudly now .
' why are you not wander around the world like you wanted to and why isn't she settling down with a family like she does?!' 
' abhi, if that is all you wanted to say , I am out of here' I said standing up.
He stood up too. Did I see pain in his eyes?
' Vicky, you are in it deep ,,- there is no way you two get out of this without one of you getting  hurt. Frankly I'd prefer if it wasn't Amy if I had a choice '
I would too. I felt myself think. But then no one was going to be hurt! 
I don't love her. Like I love her but I don't love love her. I just like being with her. And part of it is because I know we have no expectations. She doesn't expect me to propose nor I do I expect her to follow me where my job takes me.
' you are crazy!' I told Abhi. 
' I wish I was wrong. I really really wish I was wrong.' he said quietly . ' think about it , vic, the longer you keep this up the harder it is going to be. She is already  falling for you, I can tell. If this isn't leading somewhere do all of us a favor and break up.' 


---

I saw him before he saw me.

He was at the bar with a glass of something amber, staring at nothing, and I did a quick sweep of the room out of habit — checking for Amy. She wasn't there. I wasn't sure what I felt about that. Relieved, probably. Easier to say the things I needed to say without her between us, absorbing everything, smoothing everything over.

I took a breath and walked toward him.

**'Hi.'**

He looked up. Didn't smile. Abhi had never smiled at me, not once in the year and a half that Amy and I had been whatever we were. He'd been polite at best, frigid at worst, and usually somewhere in between — that particular brand of civil that is functionally identical to hostile. Amy always said *that's just how he is, he warms up eventually*, and I'd stopped pointing out that so far, he hadn't.

I sat down without being invited. Ordered a beer. He watched me do it.

We'd never been alone together before. Not really. Amy was always there — talking, filling space, finding the soft tissue between two people and keeping it soft. Without her the silence had teeth.

**'So,'** he said finally. **'You're going on the trek.'**

I'd been expecting this.

The Himalayan trek had been on Amy's list for years — she'd described it to me once, her face doing that thing it does when she talks about something she wants badly, bright and slightly embarrassed about being bright. Tents. Sleeping bags. Miles of climbing. Every summer she'd talked about going and every summer something had changed her mind. I'd never asked what.

**'Yeah,'** I said. **'We enrolled last month.'**

**'She's wanted to do that for five years,'** Abhi said. He was looking at his glass. **'I managed to talk her out of it every summer.'** A pause. **'And then you showed up.'**

Here it comes, I thought. I kept my voice easy.

**'Look, I know it's not the safest — '**

**'I think it's good,'** he said.

I stopped.

He looked at me steadily. **'I mean it. It means a lot to her that you're going. That you're willing to go.'**

I genuinely didn't know what to do with that. From Abhi it landed like a small, controlled explosion — words shaped like praise arriving from a face that had never offered it before. I searched for the catch. There had to be a catch.

**'Thanks,'** I said carefully.

**'So why are you?'**

**'Why am I what?'**

**'Going.'** He said it simply, no edge in it yet. Just a question. **'It's not exactly your type of holiday. You've done enough real expeditions to know what five days in a tent actually feels like.'**

**'She wanted to go.'**

**'Right.'**

**'And if she's going, it's better she's not going alone.'**

**'Right,'** he said again. Same tone. Like he was noting something down somewhere and didn't want me to see him do it.

**'What?'** I said.

**'Nothing.'** He picked up his glass. Set it back down. **'I thought you two had an understanding.'**

And there it was.

**'We do,'** I said. My voice came out flatter than I intended.

**'Temporary arrangement. No strings.' He wasn't being sarcastic — that was the worst part. He was just repeating it back like he was checking the math. 'She has her life planned out, you have yours. Very sensible. Very adult.'**

**'Something like that.'**

**'So why are you still together?'**

**'It's been a year and a half, Abhi, it's not — '**

**'I know how long it's been.'** Something shifted in his voice. Quieter now. **'Why haven't you left? The job that was going to take you somewhere else — you're still here. The arrangement that wasn't going anywhere — you're enrolling in treks together. You're meeting her friends for drinks.'** He gestured between us with his glass. **'When exactly is the temporary part supposed to start?'**

I felt something tighten in my chest and identified it as irritation. I was almost certain it was irritation.

**'We enjoy each other's company,'** I said. **'That's not a crime.'**

**'No.'** He looked at me. **'But I think you're in love with her.'**

**'Oh, come on — '**

**'And I think you know it,'** he said, **'and you're waiting for the arrangement to expire so you don't have to decide anything. Except it isn't expiring. So here we are.'**

I stood up. **'If that's all — '**

**'Sit down, Vic.'**

I don't know why I sat down. I was taller than him. I didn't owe him anything. But there was something in the way he said it — not commanding, just — tired. Like a man who had already had this conversation with himself many times and was exhausted by the approaching repeat of it.

I sat down.

He was quiet for a moment. When he spoke again his voice was different — lower, without the argument in it.

**'She's already in it,'** he said. **'I can see it every time your name comes up. She thinks she isn't — she's very committed to the version of this where she isn't — but she is. And the longer this goes on...'** He stopped. Turned his glass slowly on the bar. **'Amy doesn't do things halfway. You probably know that by now. When she loves something she loves it completely. And when it ends she doesn't recover quickly.'**

I said nothing. I was looking at the bar and listening to him and trying not to think about the particular way she'd laughed at something I said last week — not the polite laugh, the real one, the one she couldn't quite contain — and how it had done something very specific to me and I hadn't examined what.

**'I'm not saying this to be cruel,'** Abhi said. **'I've been where you are. I understand the appeal of a version of things where nobody has to decide anything. But that isn't actually what's happening here. You know it isn't.'**

**'You don't know what's happening,'** I said. My voice came out quieter than I'd planned.

**'Then correct me.'**

I opened my mouth. Closed it.

He waited. That was the thing about Abhi — he could wait. He had an absolute patience to him, the kind that comes from genuinely not being afraid of silence. He just sat there and let the space sit between us and didn't fill it for me.

And in the space I had the uncomfortable experience of watching my own arguments fail to form.

*I don't love her*, I thought. *I like her. I like being with her. I like the way she talks and the way she thinks and the way she makes things feel uncomplicated that were previously complicated. I like that she doesn't need me to be something I'm not. I like that she laughs at the things I actually find funny and not the things I'm supposed to find funny. I like —*

I stopped.

**'Look,'** I said. **'Even if you were right — which you're not — what exactly are you asking me to do?'**

**'I'm asking you to think about what you're doing.'** He looked at me directly, and there was something behind it I hadn't expected — not hostility, not the cold assessment I'd grown used to. Something that looked almost like pain. **'If this isn't going anywhere, end it now. Before it gets harder. Because I promise you, the longer you leave it — '** He stopped. Looked away. **'The harder it is to walk away from her. For either of you.'**

He said *either of you*. He said it like he knew something about that. From the inside.

I thought about asking. I didn't.

**'I'll think about it,'** I said finally.

He nodded. Just once. Like that was the only thing he'd actually needed to hear.

We didn't say much after that. We finished our drinks. When I left he was still at the bar, staring at nothing again, turning his glass slowly in his hands, and he looked — for the first time since I'd known him — not like someone who was angry with me, but like someone who was grieving something quietly, and had been for a while.

I didn't sleep well that night.

*Either of you*, I kept thinking.

*The harder it is to walk away from her.*

I stared at the ceiling for a long time, not examining anything, which was the same as examining everything.
