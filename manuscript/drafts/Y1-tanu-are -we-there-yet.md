The early morning roads were free of traffic as Abhi kept a steady, focused hand on the wheel. We were heading back to Pune for the weekend, but the energy in the car felt completely different this time.

I sat in the passenger seat, adjusting the air vent so the breeze blew away from my face. In the rearview mirror, I could see Leo and Ash stretched out across the back row.

When Abhi pulled up outside her building, Amy was already waiting by the curb, a bright yellow tote bag resting against her ankles. Before Abhi could even put the car fully in park, Leo pushed his way out of the back seat.

"I've got it, I've got it," he muttered, taking the heavy bag from her hands and swinging it into the boot.

"Thanks, Leo!" Amy chirped, ducking into the back seat first.

"Hi, Tanu," she said to me as Leo slid in after her and shut the door.

I glanced back as Abhi pulled away from the curb. Amy was settled squarely between Leo and Ash, but both boys were sitting with their shoulders thrown out wide, deliberately crowding her space.

"Stop it, you two!" Amy grumbled.

"What?" Leo asked innocently.

"You're doing it on purpose! Give me some space," Amy complained.

"We're just sitting," Ash said.

"You're squeezing me!"

"You're fine," Leo said, laughing.

"Move!"

"No."

"Leo!"

In the rearview mirror, I saw Amy turn to her left to poke Ash sharply in the ribs, then spin around to jab her thumb into Leo's arm.

"Ow! Ow!" Ash yelped, leaning his head back with an exaggerated wince, while Leo just snickered and nudged his shoulder into hers even more.

I caught Abhi's eye. A soft smirk played on his lips before he raised his voice to address the back seat. "If you keep fighting back there, I am going to stop this car right on the side of the road."

Abhi shook his head, eyes still on the road. "I swear, I'm turning this car around."

"Why do you let Amy join us, Abhi? It is so much better without her," Ash complained.

"She is so tiny, yet she needs so much space," Leo added.

Amy was still sitting squished between the two of them, poking them with her elbows.

"Be nice, you two," Abhi warned.

"Ask her to be nice," Ash said.

I burst out laughing.

Abhi gave him a look in the mirror. "Keep talking."

Ash wisely shut up.

But they still kept bickering in low voices.

I opened the snack bag. "Okay. Everyone gets one thing."

Three hands immediately appeared between the seats.

I handed Ash the chips. Leo got the biscuits. Amy got the chocolate.

"That's bribery," Abhi said.

"You've got any other ideas?" I asked.

There was instant peace.

By the time we hit the main stretch of the expressway, the chatter in the back began to taper off. I glanced behind me twenty minutes later, and all three of them were completely asleep.

The back seat looked like a daycare nap time. Amy had nodded off completely, her head resting heavily against Leo's shoulder. Down below, her feet were slung casually across Ash's lap, and the whole scene was so ridiculous I had to bite back a laugh.

I turned back to Abhi and leaned in slightly, a soft, fond smile spreading across my face. He glanced at me, his eyes softening as he caught my smile, and we shared a quiet, amused look before turning our attention back to the road.
