The apartment was suffocatingly quiet when we finally walked through the front door. Everything inside looked completely foreign to me—a brightly lit, strange space that everyone kept telling me was my home.  
As I hovered near the entryway, staring hesitantly at the unfamiliar hallway, Abhi put a gentle, steady hand on my shoulder. "We’ll give you a proper tour tomorrow morning, Amy," he said softly, his tone careful and protective. "It’s late, and you’ve had a really long day. No need to stress yourself out trying to process everything tonight."  
I nodded in silent relief. Abhi guided me toward the main bedroom while Leo and Nick quietly brought my bags inside. But because the room felt like a stranger's house filled with six years of a life I couldn't remember, Abhi pulled up a plush armchair in the corner of my bedroom, settling in so I’d have a familiar face nearby if I woke up panicked. Leo lingered in the doorway for just a second, his dark eyes watching me with a raw, aching tenderness that made my chest tighten, before he quietly closed the door and stayed outside.  
Around two in the morning, I woke up. My throat felt parched and raw, a dry tightness nagging at me. I peeked over at the armchair; Abhi was fast asleep, his breathing slow and steady. Trying not to make a sound, I carefully slipped out from under the covers, my bare feet padding softly across the floor.  
I turned the doorknob as gently as I could and pulled the door open.
Sitting right outside, resting his back against the wall, was Leo.  
His knees were pulled up tight toward his chest, his arms folded over them, and his head was tucked into his arms, fast asleep right there on the hard floor. He looked exhausted, the sheer weight of the last few days visible in every line of his posture.  
Before I could even step through the doorway, the slight creak of the floorboards registered. Leo snapped awake instantly. He lifted his head, hastily rubbing his eyes, and scrambled to his feet the moment he saw me standing there.  
"Amy?" his voice was low, raspy with sleep. "Are you okay? Do you need something?" 
 I stared at him, my breath catching. "Leo... were you sleeping on the floor outside?"He cleared his throat, looking slightly self-conscious as he smoothed down his shirt. "I just... wanted to be close by. In case you woke up, or needed anything, or felt scared. I didn't want you to wake up feeling alone."  
 A sudden wave of warmth and gratitude washed over me. Even though six whole years of our life together were completely blank in my mind, this man—my husband—seemed so genuinely loving, so deeply devoted.  
 "I was just looking for a glass of water," I admitted softly. "My throat is really dry."  A subtle, soft shift eased the tension in Leo’s face. "Come to the kitchen," he said gently, motioning down the hall. "Water first. But... I also happen to have a tub of Belgian chocolate ice cream in the freezer. I know you can never resist ice cream, no matter what time of night it is.
 A small, genuine smile managed to break through my exhaustion. "Ice cream sounds amazing, actually.
 He poured me a glass of water in the kitchen before scooping ice cream into two small bowls. Instead of sending me back to bed, we walked into the living room and sat together on the spacious couch, the faint glow of the streetlights outside casting long, quiet shadows across the room.  
 As I took a bite, my eyes began to wander around the living room. Now that I wasn't hyperventilating, I actually took in my surroundings. The apartment was vibrant—filled with rich colors, mismatched textured cushions, hand-painted ceramic pots, and quirky decor items bursting with personality.  
 "This room..." I whispered, looking around at the bright, eclectically decorated space. "It’s so full of life. Who decorated all of this?" 
  Leo offered a quiet, tender smile, looking around the room as if seeing it through my eyes. "You did," he said softly. "Every single piece. You picked out every cushion, every rug, every weird little decoration. You spent months dragging me through flea markets and antique shops until this place felt like you."  
  I lowered my spoon, a sharp knot of guilt tightening in my throat. "I'm so sorry, Leo," I murmured, looking down at my bowl. "I look at all of this, and I look at you... and I feel so terrible that I can't remember any of it. I'm so sorry."  
  Leo set his bowl on the coffee table and turned toward me, his expression carrying a depth of raw emotion that made my heart ache.  
  "I won't lie to you, Amy," he said, his voice quiet and tight. "It hurts. The sheer effort of sitting across from you right now, looking at you, and knowing you don't remember us... it breaks my heart."  
  He paused, swallowing hard, but he didn't look away."But I will wait," he continued softly, his gaze steady and firm. "I will wait as long as it takes, Amy. Days, months, years—it doesn't matter. You are worth it. We are worth it."  
  I looked into his eyes and knew, without a shadow of a doubt, that he meant every single word. The knot of fear inside me loosened just a little bit, replaced by a quiet, grounding truth: even if my past was completely gone, the man sitting beside me loved me enough to help me find my way back.
