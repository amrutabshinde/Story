The evening before I was scheduled to fly back to Mumbai, Nick, Amy, and I were sitting around the low dining table in her Bangalore apartment.

Nick was leaning back, picking at his plate, when he turned to Amy with a slow, sly grin. “He was asking about you, Amy.”

Amy paused, a piece of flatbread halfway to her mouth, her eyebrows pulling together in genuine confusion. “Who?”

“My friend from the birthday party,” Nick said smoothly, watching her. “Asked if you’d be interested in going out for coffee sometime.”

“Oh,” Amy said softly, her voice dropping.

My throat went entirely dry. The bite of food in my mouth tasted like sand. Some guy from Nick’s party was trying to ask Amy out on a date.

“Come on,” Nick pushed, chewing loudly and gesturing with his fork. “You need to start dating again.”

“Nick, I’m not sure…” Amy muttered, biting her lower lip as her gaze drifted down to her lap, her fingers nervously picking at the edge of her placemat.

“You’ve got to get back in the game,” Nick insisted, entirely oblivious to the sudden drop in room temperature. Then, turning his head toward me, he tossed out the one question I had zero desire to answer: “What do you think, Leo? Should she start dating again?”

Of course I don’t want her to go on a date, you absolute idiot. Why did he have to drag me into this?

Both of them were looking at me—Nick with offhand curiosity, and Amy with a quiet, guarded intensity that made my skin prickle. I cleared my throat, forcing my voice into an even, casual tone that cost me every ounce of control I possessed.

“Well… if Amy wants to go, she can.”

The moment the words left my lips, the air shifted. Amy’s eyes snapped to mine, turning ice-cold. It wasn’t just disappointment; it was a sudden, sharp anger, as if I had just slapped her.

“See? Even Leo thinks you should!” Nick boomed, thoroughly pleased with himself.

“Well then,” Amy said, her voice dropping into a dangerous, icy velvet as she locked her eyes onto mine. “If the great Leo himself says so…” She gave me a look that could have stripped paint off the walls. “Fine. I’m free next weekend.”

“Perfect!” Nick chimed in, immediately pulling out his phone and tapping away. “Do you have something good to wear? Actually, never mind—we’ll go shopping.”

She barely spoke to me for the rest of the evening, offering only polite, monosyllabic responses when strictly necessary. When it was time for me to head to the airport for my flight back to Mumbai, I wrapped my arms around her in a goodbye hug. She hugged me back, but she felt distant, her head tucked low. As I pulled away, her eyes looked heavy and shadowed, and the memory of that sad, silent face followed me all the way through security.

I didn’t sleep properly for the next five days.

All through the week in Mumbai, my brain ran through every terrible permutation of Saturday night. What if she likes him? What if they keep seeing each other? What if she moves on? What if they get married? By Friday night, I couldn't take it anymore. I booked a late-afternoon flight to Bangalore for Saturday. I had to stop it.

I have some work over there on the weekend. Can I crash at your place? I texted her from the gate.

A few minutes later, her reply came back: Yeah, sure.

When I reached her apartment complex late Saturday afternoon, my pulse was hammering in my ears. I knocked on her door, terrified that I was already too late.

The door opened, and the breath was knocked straight out of my lungs.

Amy was standing in the doorway, mid-preparation. She was wearing an off-shoulder red dress that cut sharply against her collarbones, paired with delicate matching earrings. Her dark hair was down, cascading in loose, soft waves over her shoulders. She had makeup on—a touch of blush, defined eyes, and a deep lipstick that drew every bit of light in the hallway straight to her face.

She looked breath-taking. Beautiful in a way that made my chest physically ache.

If I let her walk out that door, I thought in absolute panic, Nick’s friend is going to ask her to marry him by dessert.

“Hi,” I managed to choke out, forcing my hands into my pockets so she wouldn’t see them shaking. “You… look nice.”

“Thanks,” she said neutrally, turning back toward the full-length mirror near the hallway. “I’m meeting Nick’s friend for dinner.”

“Oh yeah. I’d forgotten,” I lied smoothly, as if I hadn't just flown hundreds of kilometers across the country specifically to sabotage this exact moment.

That struck a chord. Amy froze, her posture snapping rigid—the exact posture she took whenever she was furious. She took a deep, measured breath, her chin tilting up.

On the dresser beside her lay a thin silver necklace. She picked it up, trying to hook the tiny clasp behind her neck, but her fingers were trembling just enough to make it impossible.

I stepped forward before I could talk myself out of it. “Let me help.”

She went still. Slowly, she turned her back to me, holding her dark hair over one shoulder to leave her neck bare.

I took the small silver chain from her fingers. Standing behind her, I could smell the soft, floral scent of her perfume filling the narrow space between us. My hands hovered over her skin, my knuckles lightly grazing her collarbone as I worked the small clasp. My heart was pounding so hard against my ribs I was certain she could feel the air vibrating.

“Amy,” I said quietly, gathering every shred of courage I had left. “You don’t have to go on a date if you don’t want to.”

“Why wouldn’t I want to?” she asked frostily, her back still to me as she let her hair fall back over her shoulders.

“I don’t want you to see anyone else,” I said softly.

“What’s it to you?” she countered, setting her jaw and looking at her reflection in the glass, deliberately avoiding my eyes.

“Amy, do I have to spell everything out to you?!”

Frustration boiled over. I reached out and caught her by the arm, turning her around to face me. I pulled a little harder than I intended, and she stumbled forward, coming to a halt flush against my chest. She was so close I could feel the heat of her breath against my collarbone.

“How can I know for sure unless you do, Leo!” she snapped.

She was trying to fake composure, but her cheeks were flushed a deep, bright pink, and tiny beads of sweat had formed near her hairline. Her dark eyes locked onto mine—a direct, breathless challenge.

Up close, the perfection of her makeup broke down into human details. I could see where her mascara had slightly smudged near the outer corner of her eye. I could see a tiny, imperfect spot on the curve of her lower lip where her lipstick hadn't quite filled in.

I stopped thinking. I leaned down and pressed my lips to hers.

For a fraction of a second, the world went dead quiet. Then, Amy let out a tiny, soft intake of air, and kissed me back.

I pulled her closer, my arm wrapping tightly around her waist as her hand slid up to wrap around the back of my neck. Years of unsaid words, late-night texts, quiet longing, and carefully guarded boundaries vanished into nothing. The apartment, the city, the date—everything dissolved. There was only the feeling of her hand in my hair and the warmth of her pressed against me.

When we finally broke apart, both of us were breathless, our foreheads resting against each other as my mind reeled.

“I… that… it was…” Amy stammered, her cheeks burning bright crimson as she tried—and failed—to bite down a massive smile.

“It was nice,” I said, unable to hide the grin spreading across my own face at how adorably flustered she was.

She blinked, looking wildly around the living room before blurting out, “I have a date.”

I burst out laughing. “Really? That’s what you’re thinking about right now?”

“Right, right… I do…” she mumbled, staring down at her bare wrist. “I’m getting late.”

“Yeah, very late,” I teased gently.

“Stop it, Leo!” She hit my shoulder playfully, and I caught her wrist, holding it lightly in my hand.

“Go on your date then,” I whispered, tilting my head.

Finally, the realization landed. “I should call Nick and cancel.”

“Cancel? Really?” I murmured, bringing her knuckles to my lips and pressing a slow kiss to her fingers before letting her go.

She blushed even deeper, turning away toward the couch. “I’ll get some water,” I offered, heading toward the kitchen to give her a second to catch her breath, though my own pulse was still racing.

From the kitchen, I listened to her pick up her phone and dial.

“Nick? Hey… yeah, I was getting ready for today,” her voice came through, hesitant at first, then rapid and flustered. “Leo was here, and we were talking, and… one thing led to another and we kissed… Yes! Yes, stop screaming!… So I know, I’m sorry. Well, not sorry. But can I cancel the date?… I owe you, Nick. Yes, we did… No… it wasn’t… Oh my god, hang up now! Bye, Nick… Bye… I’m hanging up… Bye!”

I walked back into the living room carrying two glasses of water. Amy was sitting on the sofa, clutching a cushion, a helpless, radiant smile plastered across her face.

I set the glasses on the coffee table and sat down beside her. Without a second thought, she leaned over and rested her head against my chest. I slipped my arm around her shoulders, pulling her flush against my side, and pressed a quiet kiss to the top of her head.

“I love you, Amy,” I whispered into her hair.

She shifted, burying her face into my shoulder. “I’ve always loved you, Leo.”

“We just never got the timing right,” I murmured.

“Until now,” she smiled, pulling herself closer into my arms.

“Finally.”
