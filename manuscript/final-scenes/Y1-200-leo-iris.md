Abhi idled the car by the curb outside an apartment building, keeping his foot lightly on the brake while the engine hummed softly in the morning heat. We were heading to Pune for the weekend, and Abhi had convinced us to let one of his new colleagues from the office tag along.

Before he even unlocked the doors, Abhi turned in the driver’s seat and pointed a stern, warning finger between me and the back seat.

“You are not to mess with her,” he told us, his voice dropping into that protective, big-brother tone he used whenever he took someone under his wing. He locked eyes with me in the side mirror. “Leo, do you understand?”

I rolled my eyes, leaning back against the passenger headrest. “Man, you can tell Ash that.”

“He doesn’t flirt like you,” Abhi shot back without missing a beat. “She’s a very good friend. I don’t want to kick you out of the house for her.”

“Fine!” I threw my hands up in mock surrender. “Cross my heart and hope to die. Happy?”

Just then, the main gate of the apartment building swung open. I glanced through the windshield and caught my first glimpse of her.

She was hard to miss. She was wearing a bright green and yellow printed dress, carrying a vivid blue purse slung over one shoulder, lugging a massive red handbag in her arm, and balancing huge, colorful earrings that dangled and swayed against her neck with every single step.

I couldn't help the dry smirk that tugged at my lips. I leaned sideways toward Abhi. “Don’t worry, Abhi. I am definitely not dating your iridescent friend.”

From the backseat, Ash let out a sudden, muffled laugh.

Abhi glared at me, nudging my shoulder with his elbow. “Be nice! Or I will kick you.”

We watched as she paused at the gate, lingering for a moment to say something animated to the building watchman. She gave the old man a warm, sunny smile, listening intently as he pointed toward the road before waving goodbye like they were lifelong friends.

She turned toward our car, her eyes brightening the moment she spotted Abhi. Abhi immediately popped the trunk, unbuckled his seatbelt, and stepped out to help load her heavy red handbag into the back.

She slid into the backseat next to Ash, bringing a sudden rush of fresh air and cheerful energy into the cramped car.

Abhi leaned in through the driver's door, pointing across the seats. “Amy, this is Leo in the front, and Ash next to you.”

“Hi,” Ash and I said in near unison.

“Hi!” she chirped, her voice instantly filling the cabin. She leaned forward between our seats with an unreserved grin. “Thank you so much for letting me tag along!”

“No problem,” Ash said easily, settling back into his seat.

I caught Ash’s eye in the rearview mirror. Ash wasn't usually quick to warm up to strangers, but looking at the relaxed, amused expression on his face, I had a distinct feeling he already liked her.

As Abhi pulled onto the highway toward Pune, the quiet, low-key mood of our morning vanished completely. Amy was a non-stop stream of conversation, effortlessly filling every lull with stories about her office work, random thoughts, and sharp, witty observations.

An hour and a half into the drive, the flat highway began to incline as we started climbing the winding curves of the Lonavala ghats. Monsoon mist hung low over the green hills, and heavy streams of water cascaded down the dark rock faces along the roadside.

The moment the waterfalls came into view, Amy practically pressed her nose against the cool glass of the window.

“Look at that!” she gasped, her voice dropping into genuine, child-like wonder. “Abhi, slow down, look at the stream on the left! Is that a waterfall right by the edge?”

“Amy, I’m driving,” Abhi chuckled, though he couldn't hide the fond, indulgent grin on his face as he checked his side mirror.

“Leo, look!” she insisted, leaning past the center console to point out my window instead. Her dangling earrings clinked lightly near my shoulder. “Have you guys driven through here during the rain before? It looks like a movie!”

Then she rolled the window down.

"Stop the car," she said.

"We're on a highway, Amy," Abhi said, already slowing down.

"Just for a second. Look at that one." She was half out the window, pointing at a waterfall.

Abhi pulled onto the shoulder. Of course he did.

She was out of the car before it had fully stopped, standing at the railing with her ridiculous earrings catching the light, watching water fall down a hillside with childish wonder. Ash got out too, wordlessly, and stood a little behind her with his hands in his pockets, watching the water. Or watching her watch the water. Hard to tell which.

I stayed in the car a minute longer than I needed to.

"She does this," Abhi said, not looking at me, watching her in the mirror instead. "Everywhere. Every time."

"Does what?"

"Finds the thing worth stopping for." He said it like a warning and an apology at once.
"This is going to be a very long trip" I said and he laughed. 
 Then he got out of the car too, and after a second, so did I.
