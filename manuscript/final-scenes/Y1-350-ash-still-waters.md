Abhi had been in the operating room for almost three hours after slicing his hand open on a broken pane and the surgeons were still working on the tendons.

Amy was sitting on the plastic chair next to mine, her knees pulled up to her chest, looking small under the harsh fluorescent lights.

My phone buzzed against my thigh for what felt like the tenth time that night.

It was Leo. Again. He was in Delhi for a three-day work conference, and being two thousand kilometers away while his best friend was in surgery was visibly unraveling him.

I swiped answer and put it to my ear before the second ring. "Leo."

"Is he out? Did the doctor come out?" Leo’s voice came through the speaker fast and ragged, stripped entirely of his usual easy confidence. "Ash, tell me what they said. Is it bad?"

"Still in surgery, man," I said quietly, keeping my tone deliberately low and even. "The nurse came out twenty minutes ago. They’re just finishing up. He’s stable."

I heard Leo let out a harsh, shaky exhale that sounded close to a dry sob. Knowing Abhi was hurt and being completely powerless to do anything about it from a hotel room was absolute torture for him.

"I should be there," Leo muttered, his voice cracking slightly. "I shouldn't have taken this stupid trip. If I was there—"

"Leo, " Amy said softly, leaning into the phone’s mic. "He’s going to be fine," , her voice impossibly steady. "Focus on getting through the night."

"I booked a six-thirty flight," Leo said. "I'll be landing in Mumbai by eight-forty. I’m coming straight to the hospital."

"We'll be here," she promised.

By ten the next morning, Abhi was propped up in a recovery room bed, his right hand thick with white gauze and heavy bandages, still groggy from the general anesthesia.

The door opened, and Leo walked in.

He looked terrible—his shirt was wrinkled from an overnight flight, the dark circles under his eyes looked like bruises, and his jaw was set so tight I thought his teeth might break.

But the exact second Abhi raised his head from the pillow, Leo’s entire posture shifted.

The tight line of his shoulders dropped. A quick, practiced smirk flickered onto his face, masking every single ounce of terror he’d carried across the country.

"Look at you," Leo said, shoving his hands into his pockets and stepping up to the side of the bed with a light chuckle. "Honestly, Abhi, if you wanted a week off work, you could have just called in sick like a normal person. You didn't have to fight a glass door."

Abhi let out a raspy, sleep-heavy laugh, shaking his head. "Shut up, Leo. The door started it."

"Right. And clearly you lost," Leo drawled, keeping his tone light and dry.

He was putting on a great performance for Abhi. He wasn't going to let Abhi feel guilty for scaring the life out of him. But standing right behind Leo, Amy and I saw right through it. We could see the faint tremor in his knuckles where he gripped the metal bed railing, and the way his eyes kept tracking the white bandages over Abhi’s wrist, making sure his friend was actually breathing, actually whole.

Amy stepped up beside Leo. She didn't say a word or interrupt his banter with Abhi. She just quietly reached down and slid her hand over Leo’s where it rested rigid against the bed rail, squeezing softly.

I stepped up on his other side, reaching out and placing a firm, grounding hand on Leo’s shoulder.
