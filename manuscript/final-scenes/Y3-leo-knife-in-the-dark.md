It was two in the morning, and the silence in the Mumbai flat was the heavy, suffocating kind that keeps you awake. Amy had been in Bengaluru for months now, and her messages had stopped coming around eight that evening. I was still staring at the ceiling when my phone lit up on the nightstand. 

Nick’s name on the screen at 2:00 a.m. is never good news.

I answered instantly, a knot twisting tight in my throat. “Hello?”

“Leo,” Nick said. His voice was wrong—strangled, shaky, stripped of its usual sharp edge. “We were attacked at my place.” 

Cold dread flooded my chest. “What? What happened?” 

He was sobbing, trying to catch ragged breaths between words. “A burglar… he had a knife. Amy took a stab in the leg. I drove her to the emergency room. She’s in surgery right now, Leo … The doctors say she’ll be fine, but I let her get hurt. God, I let her get hurt.”

“I’m coming,” I said, forcing the words through a throat that felt like glass. “I’m on the first flight out.” I swallowed hard. “Nick… what about you? Are you alright?” 

“I have a cut on my arm, but I’m fine,” he mumbled.

Only later, from the duty nurse in Bengaluru, did I find out what *fine* meant. The knife had sliced deep through his forearm, severing muscle and leaving him drenched in his own blood. He had refused to let a single paramedic touch him until Amy was wheeled into the operating theater. *“Not me,”* he had barked at the ER staff. *“Fix her first.”*  He had stayed upright on pure willpower just long enough to call me and make sure I was on my way —and collapsed cold on the hospital floor from blood loss the second he ended the call. 

That was Nick. Amy first, everyone else second, himself last. 

***

The morning flight to Bengaluru felt like an eternity. I sat in the window seat with my hands clenched into fists, staring into the dark clouds and praying to every god I’d ever ignored. 

By six a.m., I was pushing through the double doors of the Bengaluru hospital. The air smelled of antiseptic and floor cleaner. Amy was out of surgery and resting in the post-op recovery room, still under general anesthesia. They wouldn't let me in yet. 

Down the corridor, Nick was sitting on a plastic chair outside the discharge ward, his left arm stitched, bandaged, and bound tight in a canvas sling. He looked hollowed out—pale, dark circles beneath his eyes, his shirt stained brown at the cuff.

I knew it wasn’t his fault. I knew he would have taken a bullet for her. But standing in that sterile hallway, watching him cradling his arm, a dark, hideous thought gnawed at the back of my brain: *Why her? Why wasn't it you instead?* 

I hated myself for thinking it, but the terror was making me ugly.

“Nick,” I said softly, stepping closer.

He looked up, his shoulders sagging with relief. “Leo. She’s still not awake.” 

“Not yet. How are you holding up?” 

“I’m fine.” He stared down at his bandaged arm, his jaw tightening. “I wish we could switch places.” 

I thought he meant with Amy—that he wished he was the one in the recovery bed. 

“I wish so too,” I said, the words slipping out harsher than I intended.

Nick let out a small, bitter laugh, looking up to catch my eye. “Amy wouldn’t have it any other way.”

I closed my eyes, the sting of his truth settling over me. He was right. Amy wouldn't have stood by and let anyone she loved take the blow. 

***

By noon, they moved Amy to a private room. 

She looked impossibly small in the center of the hospital bed , hooked up to IV drips and heart monitors, a yellowing bruise marking her forehead. Her leg was elevated, wrapped in thick layers of gauze. 

Nick walked in behind me. He didn't say a word; he just stepped up to the foot of the bed and gently adjusted the blanket over her feet, making sure her bare toes weren't cold. Then he sank into the corner armchair, pulled his knees up, and put his face in his hand. 

“She jumped in front of me,” Nick whispered, his voice breaking as quiet tears leaked through his fingers. “I should have seen him, Leo. I should have protected her. It’s all my fault.”

“Nick, stop,” I said, sitting on the edge of her mattress and taking her limp hand in mine. “You did everything you could.” 

I meant it. Whatever friction or envy I sometimes felt over how easily they understood each other, I knew Nick loved her with everything he had. They shared a bond that felt almost ancient, like two halves of the same chaotic frequency. 

On the bed, Amy stirred. Her eyes stayed closed, but her face twitched. It was painful to see her so still and quiet.
She kept sleeping. 

I eventually dozed off on the sofa. Nick stayed in the chair. When Amy finally woke, it was Nick who noticed first and gently shook me awake.

She opened her eyes, blinking against the dim lights. When she saw us, a weak smile touched her lips.  

Both of us stood up instantly. 

She blinked against the sharp fluorescent lights. For a second, her gaze was unfocused, confused. Then she saw us standing over her, and a frail, hesitant smile touched her lips.

“Hi…” she rasped, her voice dry.

“Hi,” I breathed, squeezing her fingers. She squeezed back weak as a bird. 

“Amy, how are you feeling?” I asked.

She opened her mouth, her face twitching slightly as the fog of anesthesia cleared and memory came rushing back. Panic flared in her eyes. She ignored me completely, her eyes darting past my shoulder to Nick.

“Nick!” she said sharply, trying to pull herself up. “He had a knife! Where did he cut you? How’s your arm?” 

Nick let out a dry, disbelieving laugh. “Amy, lie back down. How are *you*?” 

“Don't dodge the question!” she insisted, her breathing hitching as she pushed herself up on one elbow. “Why is your hand in a sling? Is it bad?” 

Nick closed his eyes for a long moment, leaning his head against the wall. He knew her too well to bother arguing.

“I’m fine,” Nick said gently. “Just a cut.”

“A few stitches,” I added, supporting her shoulder so she wouldn't strain her back. “They discharged him hours ago. He just needs to rest his arm.”

“How are *you* feeling, Amy?” Nick asked again.

“I’m feeling good. Nothing’s hurting,” she replied, a little too quickly. Nick shot me a knowing look.  

Nick shot me a knowing look that said, she was hiding something.

“Right,” Nick said dryly. He straightened his sling and tapped the doorframe. “I’ll give you two a minute.”

The moment the wooden door clicked shut behind him, the smile vanished from Amy’s face. 

Her eyes closed, her body going entirely limp against the pillows. A tiny, shuddering gasp escaped her chest.

I leaned down, lifting her hand to my lips. “Ever since you came into my life,” I whispered, “I swear I lose ten years of my lifespan every few months.”

She gave a tiny, pitiful whimper. 

“Leo…”  her voice breaking down into raw, unvarnished truth now that Nick wasn't there to hear it. “It hurts. It hurts so much.” 

My stomach dropped. “I’m calling the nurse —”

“Leo, wait,” she choked out, her fingers tightening on mine with terrifying strength. She looked at me, her eyes wide, glossy, and filled with a quiet, childlike terror. “Am I… am I going to be able to walk?” 

It was the question she’d been holding in her throat from the second she woke up, the fear she couldn't afford to show Nick. 

My heart broke right there in my chest. I leaned down, resting my forehead gently against hers, letting her feel my breath, my presence, my absolute certainty.

“Of course you are,” I told her, my voice steady and iron-firm. “The doctor said the muscle damage is clean. You’re going to heal up fine and be out of here in a couple of days.” 

I was bending the truth on the timeline, but she needed to hear it. 

“Really?” she whispered, the tension draining out of her shoulders. 

“Really. You might have a slight limp for a for the rest of you life, but don't worry—I’ll carry you everywhere” I joked. And before he she could react. "I am kidding. You are going to walk out form here yourself." I kissed her temple gently. “Now let me get the doctor.” 

When I pulled open the door, Nick was standing right outside in the corridor, leaning against the wall with his good hand resting on the call button. He raised his eyebrows at me.

“So?” Nick asked quietly.

“She’s in severe pain,” I said. 

“Doctor’s already on his way,” Nick said, nodding down the hall where a nurse was already walking toward us with a syringe. “Called him three minutes ago.” 

Of course he had. 

A moment later, the nurse administered the booster into Amy’s IV line. Nick and I stood side by side at the foot of her bed as the sedative began to circulate. Amy stared up at the plastic drip chamber, frowning as if she could force the medicine to work faster by sheer force of will.

“It takes some time to kick in,” Nick said softly, crossing his good arm over his chest. 

Amy glanced up at him, caught in her lie, looking thoroughly sheepish.

“No more lies,” Nick told her, his tone stern but warm.

Amy glared at him weakly through heavy eyelids. “No more 'It's all my fault' crying in the corner, then.”
Nick flinched. So she had heard him last night. 

He offered a small, tired smile. “Fair enough. Deal.”

I looked from Amy, smiling triumphantly throught he pain, to Nick, standing guard beside her bed despite bleeding through his own bandages. 

*Idiots,* I thought to myself, feeling a strange, aching warmth bloom in my chest. *Both of them. Complete idiots.*
