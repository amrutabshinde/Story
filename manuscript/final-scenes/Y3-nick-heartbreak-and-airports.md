The departures board glowed with the word Delayed, staring down at us with the stubborn confidence of something that had no intention of changing anytime soon. It was past midnight at the Bangalore airport, and Amy and I were stranded on our way to Mumbai.

Amy, true to form, had scouted out a secluded corner. We dumped our luggage, claimed a couple of metal seats next to a charging station, and settled in for the long haul.

When her phone buzzed, I watched her look down. A familiar, soft smile ghosted across her face.

"Who is it?" I asked, though I was ninety-nine percent sure I already knew.

"Leo." Bingo.

I crunched loudly on a potato chip.

"What?" she asked, looking up.

"I didn't say anything."

"You're making a face, Nick."

"I'm eating chips. This is just my chip face."

With a roll of her eyes, she turned the screen toward me. Have you eaten. No question mark. Just a quiet, authoritative demand. I looked from the screen back to her. She was already typing a response, that subconscious, gentle expression settling over her features again.

For three months, I’d watched Leo text her from Mumbai. He texted about her meals, her sleep schedule, whether her auto driver looked trustworthy. It was as if she occupied a permanent residency in his thoughts.

"Amy," I said quietly.

"He worries about everyone, Nick," she countered instantly. She didn't even lift her eyes from the screen. She’d clearly had this exact argument with herself so many times that the defense was automated.

"Right. Which is why every time he comes to Bangalore for work, he somehow spends the entire weekend with you. I’ve literally never seen the guy do a single stroke of actual work."

"I know." Her voice dropped, flat and final, like a heavy door shutting. "Nick, I know."

She locked her phone and stared out at the tarmac. "When I hurt my back, he wouldn't let me live alone. He packed up my entire life and brought me to his house so he and Ash could nurse me back to health. He notices the stupidest things—like if I skip my morning tea, he will make it and come by my office to bring me a cup. He notices things I don't even realize about myself, and then he quietly goes out of his way to fix them."

"Then why are you putting up walls?" I asked, looking at her closely.

"Because." She stopped, the bright, chaotic lights of the departure board reflecting off the lenses of her glasses. "Because last year, I let myself believe it meant something. So when he asked me out to that nice dinner, I thought .... I thought it was a date." She swallowed, her throat tight. "Turns out, he brought me there along with Abhi and Ash to introduce us to his new girlfriend, Nat."

I slowly lowered the bag of chips. "Ouch. Amy, I'm sorry."

"So that’s Leo," she said. Her voice was terrifyingly even, completely detached from the pain of the memory. "And I am never, ever falling for it again."

I understood the instinct to protect yourself. God knows I’d had my own share of disappointments—sometimes it feels safer to expect the worst so the universe can’t surprise you. But looking at the situation from the outside, something didn't line up. Leo wasn't dating anyone now. He was constantly hovering, finding every flimsy excuse to fly to Bangalore just to breathe the same air as her. It didn't look like casual worrying; it looked like a guy who was desperately homesick for a person.

Before I could say any of it, Amy let out a breathless, self-deprecating laugh. "I just have the absolute worst luck. It’s like I’m biologically cursed when it comes to crushes."

"Come on, it can't be that bad," I tried to tease, wanting to bring back her usual warmth.

But she didn't laugh. She just looked down at her hands, and for the first time, the bright, bubbly mask she always wore began to slip.

"It is, though," she whispered, her voice cracking just a fraction. "It’s exhausting, Nick. People see me smiling and laughing, and they think I just bounce back. But it takes so much energy to keep pretending it doesn't break a piece of you every single time."

She took a slow, shaky breath.

"Leo came along, and I fell so incredibly hard. I thought we just clicked. We were inseparable. And then came that dinner... and the realization that I was entirely invisible to him in the way that actually mattered."

She looked up at me, her eyes bright with unshed tears, though her lips tried to force a small, painful smile.

"And then... then there was Vic. We were never meant to become so serious. He moved to Europe, and my whole world just collapsed. I couldn't breathe in Mumbai anymore. Every street, every coffee shop, every corner felt like a ghost of someone who chose to leave me behind. That's the real reason I fled to Bangalore, Nick. It wasn't a career move. I was just running away because I couldn't bear to look at a city that kept breaking my heart. And Leo was there when Vic left, so he fusses over me now to make sure I am okay."

The sheer weight of what she had been carrying hit me all at once. Behind every joke, every bright laugh, and every cheerful piece of advice she’d given me over the past few months, there was this deep, quiet ache of constantly feeling chosen second. Or not chosen at all.

"Are you okay?" I asked, my voice softer now, the teasing completely gone.

She blinked away the moisture in her eyes, her characteristic resilience pulling itself back together like armor. "I will be," she said, giving me a firm, determined nod.

"Okay," I replied, nudging her shoulder with mine.

"Anyway!" Amy cleared her throat, her voice suddenly bouncing back to its usual, animated pitch as she forcefully changed the subject. "Look at you, just sitting there, gloating over my tragic, failed love life."

"I would never gloat," I said, entirely defensive.

"Oh, please." She gestured at me comprehensively. "Nick, look at you. You’ve probably had girls throwing themselves at your feet since you were fifteen. With that face and those eyes? You can literally have anyone you want. Look at the girls across the terminal—they’ve been ogling you for twenty minutes and giving me the stink-eye because they're jealous." She shoved the bag of chips back into my hands with a laugh. "You don't have a clue what it’s like to love someone who doesn't even see you."

I held the bag of chips, the plastic crinkling under my fingers. I didn't eat. I didn't laugh.

"Nick?" she asked, her smile fading a little when she noticed my silence.

I had never spoken these words aloud. Not to my family, not to my friends, not even to the mirror. But looking at Amy—seeing how much she had risked by being honest—made me want to share the weight.

"There's someone," I said quietly.

Amy went perfectly still.

"We grew up together in Mumbai," I said, keeping my eyes fixed on the airport carpet. It was a miserable, corporate shade of brown that looked like it had given up on life. "She's my dad's best friend's daughter. I think... I think I've loved her my entire life. We've always been incredibly close. Even when my family packed up and moved to Bangalore, we never lost touch. For me, it was always just a given. There was never anyone else." I paused, taking a breath. "I’ve known her since we were kids. But she doesn't see me that way. Last year, she got engaged to someone else."

I felt Amy’s hand slip into mine, squeezing tightly.

"What's her name?" she asked, her voice incredibly gentle.

"Miles."

Then, trying to mimic her ability to find a silver lining, I looked up and smiled brightly. "But, hey, on the positive side... she broke off the engagement."

"Really?" Amy’s face instantly lit up, that fierce, unyielding optimism she reserved for everyone else suddenly flooding back into her eyes.

"Yeah. There was a ton of drama, so she actually went to London to study and clear her head. But she came back to Mumbai last month. And I'm going to see her this weekend."

"Are you going to ask her out?" Amy grinned, throwing both arms around my elbow and leaning into my shoulder.

"Maybe. Let's see." I looked down at her. Honestly, for the longest time, I had been terrified to tell Miles how I felt. I didn't want to risk losing my oldest friend and making things weird. But since I’d met Amy, the world felt a little less lonely, and the fear didn't feel quite so paralyzing. "I think I might actually do it. I might ask her on a real date."

Right on cue, her phone buzzed again.

Amy chuckled, turning the screen toward me.

Chips are still not food. Please eat something real.

I watched as Amy typed back: Goodnight Leo.
A second later, his reply flashed: Eat something.
She locked the phone with a decisive click.

"Tell you what," Amy said, looking at me thoughtfully. "If this weekend goes south and it doesn't work out with Miles... we should probably just get married."

I choked on my own breath, laughing. "Are you proposing to me right now at a airport depature gate?"

"Ew, no," she said, wrinkling her nose playfully. "Not like that. We can just be each other’s backup plans."

"No, thank you."

"Oh, come on!" she insisted, her eyes wide with sudden excitement. "Given my absolute track record of disaster in the love department, there is literally no one else I’d rather grow old and miserable with. Think about it."

"We don't need a marriage certificate for that, Amy. We can just be there for each other."

"Okay, fine, or we can have a totally modern, open marriage," she countered wildly. "You can sleep with whoever you want, I don't care!"

I stared at her, genuinely shocked by the chaotic direction her brain was taking. "You seriously need to stop binging sitcoms. They are actively rotting your brain."

Instead of backing down, she scrambled up, sitting cross-legged on her metal airport seat to face me directly. She grabbed both of my shoulders, her eyes alight with a manic, imaginative energy.

"Nick, please! Just imagine when I post our wedding pictures on Facebook. All the girls from my high school will literally drop dead of envy. What is the point of you having those ridiculous blue eyes and Greek-god cheekbones if you can't even use them to help a friend out?"

I stared at her for a long beat, utterly exhausted but entirely charmed. "If I agree to this, will you finally leave me alone and let me sleep?"

"Yes! Yes, I swear," she said, crossing her heart.

I let out a long, dramatic sigh. "Fine. I’ll be your backup husband."

"Yes!" Amy squealed, leaning forward to plant a quick, affectionate peck on my cheek. "Husband, we are absolutely changing the curtains in our future house to bright florals. The ones you have in your apartment right now are depressing."

I buried my face in my hands, my shoulders shaking with laughter. Beside me, Amy was laughing just as hard.

Looking at her, I thought that maybe it wasn't the worst backup plan in the world. And as the board finally flashed a boarding gate for Mumbai, I realized that whatever happened next, neither of us had to carry our heartbreaks alone anymore.
