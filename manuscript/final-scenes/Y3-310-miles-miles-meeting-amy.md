"Have you considered," Amy said, batting her eyelashes with what I could only describe as reckless commitment, "that I could pretend to be your girlfriend?"

I looked at her.

She looked back at me, entirely hopeful.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Several reasons. First—whatever you're doing with your eyes right now is going to give me nightmares."

Amy looked deeply offended.

"Second," I continued before she could recover, "I don't think she'd care."

She stopped walking right in the middle of the garden path. "What?"

"I said—"

"I heard what you said."

We started moving again. The garden path opened up ahead of us, flowers blooming along the fence line and people gathered out on the patio. I could already hear the faint jingling of a collar.

Amy looked me up and down with the critical expression of someone conducting a property survey. "Have you had a look at yourself recently?"

"In what sense?"

"In the sense that nobody could not fall for that."

I laughed. I couldn't help it. "Thank you. That's somehow both flattering and deeply concerning."

"I'm serious."

"She has conveniently friend-zoned me for approximately forever."

As the lawn opened up completely ahead of us, Amy’s eyes suddenly lit up with pure mischief. Before I could even react, she grabbed my arm and forcefully slung it around her own shoulders.

"Amy," I hissed under my breath, trying to pull away.

"Just testing!"

"You are an absolute menace."

Then I spotted Miles standing near the far end of the garden, watching the two of them with her eyebrows distinctly raised. I immediately yanked my arm back. Amy looked entirely too pleased with herself—a little devil in a sun dress.

"Miles," I said, offering a warm smile as we approached.

"Nick," she replied, her smile tight and her eyes flicking between us.

"This is Amy," I started, but before I could finish the introduction, Amy dashed forward and wrapped her arms completely around Miles in a sudden, enthusiastic hug.

"It is so good to finally meet you! Nick talks about you all the time!" Amy chirped.

"Ah. Does he now?" Miles said, freezing completely under the unexpected affection, looking like she suddenly didn't know what to do with her own hands and feet.

I opened my mouth to contribute something useful to break the tension, but I never got the chance. A large, ecstatic blur of golden fur shot straight across the lawn.

Amy barely had time to squeak before the dog launched his entire body weight at her.

"Oh my God!"

"Mont!" Miles shouted, lunging forward.

Too late. The dog had already decided that Amy was the greatest thing that had ever happened to him. She was knocked down to her knees within seconds, but instead of being upset, she burst into a bright, ringing laugh. Mont was ecstatic. There was frantic licking. There was breathless giggling.

"Hello, hello!" Amy squealed as the dog put his entire face into her hair.

Lick.

"Hello!"

Lick.

"Oh, you're beautiful!"

Mont's tail wagged so hard his entire back half vibrated.

"Traitor," Miles muttered, looking down at her own dog with a mix of betrayal and amusement.

The laughter carried easily across the quiet garden. I watched Amy for a moment—properly, the way you watch someone without meaning to. On the flight over, she had told me things. Things she carried quietly, the kind of heavy emotional weight you'd miss entirely if you didn't know exactly where to look. Worries she'd flattened into mere practicalities; fears she'd presented as minor inconveniences.

Yet right now, she was on her knees in the grass laughing because a dog had completely buried his face in her hair, looking as if none of that weight had ever touched her. My gaze stayed a second longer than I'd planned.

Then a shoulder nudged mine, breaking my train of thought.

"I didn't know you were bringing someone," Miles said quietly, standing beside me.

I looked down at her, catching the subtle, defensive stiffness in her posture. A slow, irresistible grin spread across my face. "Are you jealous?"

She scoffed immediately, rolling her eyes. "Please."

The response came out far too quickly, and she knew it.

"I didn't even know you were coming until this morning," she added, throwing as much casual indifference into her voice as humanly possible. The sharp emphasis suggested she very much did not care. At all. Not even slightly.

"Mm," I hummed, thoroughly enjoying myself.

She narrowed her eyes at me.

Deciding to let her off the hook for a second, I reached out and picked up a homemade walnut cookie from the plate resting on the garden table. I took a bite, chewed, and then stopped entirely. The texture was perfect. "These are incredible."

Miles went completely still.

I turned toward the house, raising my voice. "Aunty!"

Her mother’s face immediately appeared at the open kitchen window, beaming down at us.

"These are amazing!" I called out to her. "They're my favorite—you know that."

"Then you should thank Miles," her mother called back cheerfully. "She was up baking them for you until midnight last night!"

An absolute, crushing silence descended on the patio.

Slowly, deliberately, I turned my head back toward Miles.

Her face had gone bright pink from the jaw up. She tried to stare me down with absolute defiance, but I just locked my eyes onto hers, completely silent, utterly amused.

"My mother," Miles squeezed out, her voice incredibly tight as she picked up the cookie plate with immense, rigid dignity, "exaggerates."

"Amy and I aren't dating," I added softly, letting a gentle, reassuring edge creep into my tone. "Just friends. In case that was... relevant."

"It wasn't," she snapped.

"Right."

"As if I care."

Turning on her heel, she marched straight toward the kitchen door, clutching the plate tight against her chest. Even from behind, I could see her ears were still burning pink. She pointedly refused to look back at me, and my smile only widened.
