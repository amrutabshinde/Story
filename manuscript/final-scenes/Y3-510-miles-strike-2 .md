For months, I had almost managed to convince myself that the food poisoning in Bengaluru had been just that—bad seafood, a careless restaurant, an unfortunate coincidence. I had spent weeks scrutinizing every detail, but as the weeks stretched on without another incident, the knot in my chest had slowly begun to loosen. I had started to breathe again.

And then my mother's phone rang.

It was Nick's mother. I stood in the doorway of the living room, watching the color drain from my mother's face as she listened.

A burglar. A knife. Amy in surgery. Nick cut deep.

Panic flared through my veins like ice water. He was in danger. It hadn't been a coincidence—it was happening again. While my parents immediately packed their bags and booked the earliest flight to Bengaluru to be at his side, I stood frozen in the hallway, paralyzed by a sheer, suffocating terror.

I couldn't go. I told my parents I was tied up with an urgent project at work, hiding behind my laptop screen while my hands shook so violently I couldn't type. The truth was far more embarrassing: I was a coward. I couldn't bring myself to walk into another hospital corridor. I couldn't bear the thought of seeing Nick broken or bleeding, forced to confront just how close I had come to losing him.

For three days, I hid behind my desk. For three days, I barely slept.

On the third morning, the guilt proved heavier than the fear. I bought a one-way ticket to Bengaluru. I moved down the hospital hallway on heavy feet until I found the room number my mother had texted me.

Amy was still inside. Outside, sitting on a stiff plastic chair against the wall, was Nick.

He was asleep, his head tilted back against the yellowing paint of the hospital wall. His left arm was bound tightly in a thick white sling, the bandages visible beneath his sleeve. His face looked drawn, shadows bruised deep under his eyes. Looking at him like that—vulnerable and worn thin—felt like a hand squeezing my heart until I couldn't breathe.

I stepped quietly across the tile and sat in the empty chair right beside him.

He felt the shift in weight beside him and stirred. His blue eyes fluttered open, dull with exhaustion, before settling on me.

"Hi," he said softly.

There was no smile. No familiar spark of mischief in his tone. Just a flat, hollow quiet.

"Hi," I whispered, my voice barely catching in my throat.

"How's Amy" I asked.

"She'll be fine. " he said noding towards the closed door. 

"How are you?"

He did not answer. 

"It's been three days," he stated simply, staring ahead at the far wall.

"Yeah."

"Your parents visited," he added.

"I know. I… I've been really busy with work," I lied, the words tasting like ash in my mouth.

Nick quieted for a long second. "Right," he said.

There was no anger in his voice. No accusation, no sharp retort. Just a quiet, heavy hurt that was infinitely worse.

I sat there, watching him. I could feel him pulling back, re-evaluating, quietly rearranging his place in my life. He was doing what he always did—assuming he wasn't important enough, assuming he didn't warrant me dropping everything to come to his side. He was demoting his own significance, preparing to swallow the disappointment without ever uttering a single complaint.

It horrified me. The thought of him slipping away into that quiet, self-sacrificing distance made my chest ache so fiercely I thought it would crack.

"Nick, please talk to me," I begged, my composure breaking.

He let out a low, tired sigh, biting his lower lip as his eyes flickered to mine—bright and gleaming with unshed pain. "After last time, I thought I knew where we stood with each other," he said softly, his voice tight. "I guess I was wrong."

"No—Nick, no," I sobbed, tears spilling hot and fast down my cheeks. I couldn't let him think that. The only thing that could fix this was the truth.

"I couldn't see you hurt again," I confessed, my breath hitching in my chest as the tears blurred my vision. "I couldn't bear to walk through those doors and see you in a hospital bed again… thinking about how close I came to losing you." A harsh, uncontrolled sob ripped from my throat. "Again."

Nick went completely still.

The guarded, stern line of his jaw slackened. The distance in his blue eyes vanished, replaced by a sudden, quiet realization. The tension drained out of his shoulders as he finally understood that my absence hadn't been indifference—it had been terror.

A faint, tender spark of surprise flickered across his face.

"Miles…" he said softly.

"I can't lose you, Nick," I cried, wiping uselessly at my cheeks, all my carefully built defenses crumbling into dust. "I just… I don't know how to live without you."

Nick stared at me for a heartbeat, his expression softening into something devastatingly gentle. A small, warm smile touched the corners of his lips.

"Come here…" he murmured.

He extended his good right arm, gathering me in and pulling me firm against his chest. I collapsed into him, burying my face in the crook of his neck, my shoulders shaking as I let go of three days' worth of suffocating panic. His solid warmth, his steady heartbeat beneath my cheek—it was the only real thing in the world.

"I really hate you a lot," I mumbled thick with tears, digging my head deeper into his chest.

Nick leaned his chin lightly against the top of my head, his good arm wrapping tighter around me.

"I know, Miles," he whispered softly, his breath stirring my hair. "I know."
