The late afternoon sun filtered through the gym window, casting long shadows across the treadmill display. I always came in at five, claiming the exact same spot by the glass for the view of the quiet compound and the small, unassuming bus stop just outside. It was usually dead, save for a few office workers heading home, until six o'clock rolled around.

That was when she would arrive.

I didn't mean to stare, but after weeks of the same routine, her presence had become a predictable landmark of my workouts. She was always immersed in a book while she waited. Sometimes , she’d slip off her shoes, leave them on the pavement, and pull her feet up onto the wooden bench to sit cross-legged. From my spot inside, I couldn't help but notice the covers of the novels she held. I recognized the distinctive spines of high fantasy—sprawling maps, gilded crests, and epic titles. Lately, she had been working her way through the newest Game of Thrones book.

I had devoured that exact same book a few weeks back, and it had left me completely reeling. A major character—someone I was convinced was meant to pull through—was brutally killed off near the end, leaving me in a state of quiet disbelief for days. As the week progressed, I found myself checking her progress from the window, watching the remaining pages thin between her fingers. I kept wondering how she would react when the blade finally fell.

On Friday evening, right on cue at six, her usual bus rumbled up to the curb. But she didn't move. She remained frozen over the open page, so deeply absorbed that the bus simply shut its doors and pulled away. She had deliberately chosen to miss it, unable to pause the story so close to the climax.

A few minutes later, I finished my workout, threw my gym bag over my shoulder, and stepped out into the cool evening air toward my car. My path took me right past the bus stop. As I approached, I saw her abruptly snap the thick book shut, set it on her lap, and bury her face in her hands.

She had just reached the end.

I hesitated, slowing my stride before walking over toward the shelter.

"Are you okay?" I asked softly.

She looked up, blinking against the light, and let out a breathless laugh. "Yes, yes," she said, gesturing vaguely at the book. "It's... it's the book. I'm trying to process what I just read. Sorry, I'm just being silly."

"I read it a few weeks back," I said, leaning slightly against the post. "And I'm still in shock about the end."

At my words, her embarrassed smile vanished, replaced by an expression of absolute, genuine betrayal. "I thought he was going to be the king!" she cried. "How could he die?!"

"I know!" I echoed, shaking my head. "It was so unfair." I paused, looking down at her. "Are you going to be alright?"

"Of course," she said, smiling as she leaned down to put her shoes back on. "I'll just eat tubs of ice cream to get over it."

I laughed, extending a hand as she stood up. "I'm Nick, by the way."

She shook it, the lingering sorrow completely replaced by a warm, bright smile. "Amy. I'm Amy."
