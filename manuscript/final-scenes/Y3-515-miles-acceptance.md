It was close to midnight when Ami called. We spent the first ten minutes going over office updates before he finally got to the real reason he’d dialled—he just wanted to check on me.

I was staying at Nick’s parents’ house. A week ago, a burglar had broken into Nick’s apartment. In the scramble to tackle the knife-wielding intruder, Amy had been stabbed in the thigh, and Nick had taken a deep gash to his arm. Nick, eaten alive by guilt, had insisted on bringing Amy here the moment she was discharged so she wouldn't be alone while recovering.

"How are you holding up, Miles?" Ami asked softly.

"I'm fine," I said.

"You don't sound fine," he replied. "Is it because Nick brought Amy home?"

I quieted, my hand tightening on the receiver. "He could have asked me, Ami. It’s not like I would have said no—we owe her everything for stepping in like that. But he should have asked."

"I agree," Ami said cautiously. "Do you think... there’s something more between them?"

"No!" I said immediately.

Ami let out a small breath, exactly as he’d intended. He knew I wasn't threatened by Amy personally; he just wanted me to say it out loud.

"They’re friends, Ami," I murmured. "I know that much."

"Then why are you so worked up?"

"Because Nick made the decision without me," I said, the frustration breaking through.

"You're not angry that Amy's here. You're angry that your boyfriend- can we call him that now? - didn't include you."

I let out a long breath.

"Then stop convincing yourself it's about Amy. Get some sleep." he continued

"I will."

After hanging up, I heard quiet murmuring from down the hall and stepped out toward the kitchen. Amy was standing near the stove, limping heavily as she moved between the counter and the electric kettle, chattering away to the house cook.

"—it’s just such a massive house, Sonal," Amy was saying, her voice a rapid, airy stream. "Every time the floorboards creak I think it’s a ghost. Do you take sugar? I stopped putting sugar in mine—trying to cut back, though honestly, it tastes like hot dishwater without it—"

She was moving too fast, talking too much. That was Amy in a nutshell: relentless chatter masking whatever was actually going on inside.

"Hi, Amy," I said, leaning against the doorframe. "Everything okay?"

She startled slightly, then offered a warm, apologetic smile. "Oh, Miles! Yes, everything’s fine. Just couldn't sleep. Want some tea?"

"I'm good, thanks," I said, watching her carefully. "Shouldn't you be off that leg?"

"I've been lying down all day," she protested, pouring tea into two mugs. She handed one to Sonal, who murmured a polite thanks and quickly excused herself. Amy limped over to the dining table with her own mug, setting it down with a quiet clink.

"To be honest," Amy admitted in a lower voice, looking down at the amber liquid, "I haven't really slept since that night. The moment the house goes quiet, I start hearing things. Don't tell Nick, though—he's already hovering enough as it is. I sleep during the day instead. It's easier when everyone's awake."

She shrugged it off as if it were nothing, taking a sip. Self-sacrificing to a fault, making sure no one else had to bear her fear.

Footsteps sounded in the hallway, and Nick walked in. His left arm was immobilised in a heavy sling, but his right hand held a plastic grocery bag. "Hello, ladies," he said.

He dumped two tubs of chocolate ice cream into the freezer, then walked over and sat beside me at the table, dropping a heavy, affectionate arm across my shoulders.

"Where did you go at midnight?" I asked.

"Someone," Nick said, nodding toward Amy, "keeps eating all the ice cream in the house."

"I get hungry at night," Amy argued defensively.

"Then eat an apple," Nick replied. Without asking, he leaned over, took Amy’s tea mug right out of her hands, took a long sip, and handed it back to her.

It was such an offhand, instinctive gesture—a quiet, effortless intimacy built from months of living in each other's pockets. Watching it, a sharp prick of jealousy flared in my chest, no matter how much I tried to reason it away.

Amy blew on her tea, then carefully pushed herself up from the chair. "I'm going to take this back to my room," she said, limping slowly toward the sink to leave her spoon.

Nick was on his feet instantly. "Let me help you."

"I can walk, Nick," she said, her tone suddenly flat.

"Amy—"

"I've got it."

"Just let me help you. Please." Nick’s voice carried a raw, pleading edge that hung heavy in the kitchen.

Amy stopped in her tracks. She turned slowly, her small frame rigid.

"Tell me something, Nick," she said, folding her arms tight across her chest. "Why do you keep acting like it was your job to protect me?"

Nick blinked, caught off guard. "What?"

"Think carefully before you answer," she warned, stepping closer despite her limp. "Because if you say it's because I'm smaller, or because I'm a woman, I swear I'll slap you right here."

"You know I don't think that," Nick said, pulling back slightly confused where this was all coming from.

"Then why are you treating me like a victim you failed?" Amy challenged, her voice rising, sharp and clear. "Why do you get to be the only hero in the room? If you had taken that knife for me, you'd be wearing it like a badge of honour. But because I did it, you walk around this house looking like you're on trial!"

"Amy, that's not—"

"It is," she snapped, cutting him off cleanly. "You think you're responsible for everything that happens to the people around you. I didn't get hurt because you failed, Nick. I got hurt because I chose to step in. I made a decision. Stop taking that away from me."

Silence settled over the kitchen, thick and sudden. Nick stood frozen, looking at her as if she’d just struck him.

Sitting at the table, the tension in my own chest shifted.

All week, I had seen their bond through the lens of my own exclusion—the private jokes, the quiet glances, the fierce protectiveness. But watching them now, the truth laid itself bare. This was Amy refusing to let Nick drown in his own martyr complex. She wasn't asking for his pity, and she certainly wasn't looking for his protection; she was demanding that he respect her choices as an equal.

Nick swallowed hard, his posture deflating. "I never meant to make you feel like that."

Amy stared at him for another second, then her rigid shoulders relaxed, the fierce spark softening into her usual dry humor. "Good. Then stop moping around the house like a tragic ghost. Everyone's so busy feeling sorry for you that nobody's properly appreciated my heroics."

Nick let out a quiet, breathless laugh, rubbing his face with his good hand. "What, you want a medal now?"

"A medal and  a parade would be nice." Amy said, turning back toward the hallway with a faint, triumphant smirk. "Now leave me alone so I can limp back to bed in peace."
