The phone had been ringing nonstop since morning. I finally managed to get up from the sofa I was sleeping on to pick it up, almost tripping over Ash, who lay snoring on the carpet. 

It was Amy.

"Hello," I croaked.

"Leo, I am going to be late. I'll be there in like half an hour." 

She hung up. It took me some time to process the information. Amy was going to be home in half an hour. My drunk roommates were passed out in the hall, and empty bottles and snack packets were scattered all across the floor.

"Wake up," I said, kicking Abhi, who was sleeping under a pile of pillows.

"Dude, stop yelling. My head hurts," he said, clutching his forehead.

"Abhi, Amy is coming here in half an hour."

"Oh man!" He sprung up like a jack-in-the-box.

Soon, the three of us had cleared the room. Abhi took a quick shower, and I was just having mine when the doorbell rang. Amy was home.

Before long, the silence of my house was broken by Amy's incessant chatter. 

I could hear Abhi and Ash hum and haw in response, but mostly, I just heard Amy's voice. *Thunder and storm*, Akki used to call her. I smiled at the memory. I could tell from the direction of the sound that they had moved into the kitchen. I changed quickly and walked toward the kitchen, only to hear Amy suddenly yelling.

"Abhi, no! Please don't!" 

I walked in to see Ash pinning Amy's hands behind her back, while Abhi held a bottle, threatening to pour it over her head. Given that Abhi and Ash were already drenched, it was clear things had escalated quickly.

"You started it!" Abhi accused.

"Leo, tell him not to—"

"Abhi, that's a bottle of cold water," I said, trying to keep a straight face. I picked up a larger water bottle from the countertop and slid it over to him. "Here, use this." 

Ash, being the traitor he is, let go of Amy's arms right then. She snatched the bottle and got her revenge by emptying it all over me. Abhi poured his water over Amy's head anyway. Then, Ash grabbed a bottle of tomato ketchup and was about to pour it on Amy when Abhi stopped him. "Dude, that's gross." Ash put it back, only to squirt it all over Abhi's head a few seconds later. 

Soon, all four of us were completely drenched and smelling of ketchup, dish soap, and some sticky, sweet substance I couldn't quite identify.

It took almost an hour to clean up the kitchen. Abhi lent Amy a long shirt to change out of her wet clothes—which made her look a bit like Yoda. She looked hilarious moving around the kitchen, fixing breakfast for us in it. Eventually, we sat on the floor eating pancakes, omelets, and sandwiches, while Amy sat on the beanbag, nibbling on her food. The TV was playing one of the Harry Potter movies. We watched it for a while before realizing Amy hadn't said anything in a long time. Abhi walked around the beanbag and smiled. Our little cat had curled up on the beanbag and fallen fast asleep.
