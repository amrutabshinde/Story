We waited in the car for Amy near her apartment gate. Leo was driving us somewhere for a surprise dinner, though he hadn’t mentioned what the occasion was, where we were going, or even if he was treating us or if we were splitting the bill. All Abhi and I knew was that Leo had asked us to keep the evening free. I assumed he’d given Amy the exact same vague instructions.

I spotted her first as she walked down the apartment stairs. Up front, Abhi and Leo were deep in a debate about some movie. Amy was wearing a pretty dress, jewelry, and a cluster of delicate bangles on her wrist—a noticeable step up from her usual effortless style.

A sudden, sickening realization hit me.

"Leo," I asked, my voice dropping, "did you tell Amy where we’re going?"

"Nah, it’s a secret," Leo said, grinning ear to ear. "Just asked if she was free for dinner tonight."

I felt a violent urge to punch him right in his perfect teeth. Amy had a massive crush on him, and he was too blind to see it. She thinks he’s asking her out on a date, I thought with a wave of sheer dread. Stupid, stupid Leo. She had no idea Abhi and I were sitting in the back seat. To spare her the immediate shock when she got to the car, I pushed my door open wide and stepped out onto the curb.

The moment she saw me, her expression shifted. Realization dawned, and she shook her head at her own folly, forcing a sheepish smile as she walked up.

"Wow, Amy, looking good!" Abhi called out from the front passenger seat.

"Stunning," Leo whistled.

"Ah," she shrugged, clicking her tongue with a dramatic flare. "I thought maybe you guys were taking us somewhere fancy." Her smile was unusually wide, even for her.

"You look nice," I murmured as she reached me.

"Thanks," she blinked, looking away quickly.

I don't think she realized that I knew how she felt about Leo. But Leo was an easy guy to fall for—unfailingly friendly, generous, and constantly flirting with her. Plus, after she hurt her back, he’d taken her in and genuinely looked after her. Amy, being Amy, was walking around with her heart completely on her sleeve.

Once she got into the car, she did her best not to let her mood sour. She kept tinkling with her bangles, occasionally shaking her head and laughing under her breath at her own mistake. The three of us kept pestering Leo to spill the real reason for the dinner, but everything was fine until he finally dropped the bomb.

"Alright, people. First off, this is a self-paid dinner and drinks," Leo announced.

"Oh, come on, man!" Abhi groaned.

"And second," Leo continued, "there’s someone I want you to meet."

 It felt like watching a disaster unfold in slow motion—you know something terrible is coming, but you're completely powerless to stop it. I glanced sideways at Amy. She had gone rigid, turning to stone.

"I met someone a few weeks ago," Leo said, glowing. "Her name is Natasha. And I want to introduce her to my best friends in the whole wide world." He blew a goofy kiss toward Abhi.

"That was the big surprise?!" Abhi grumbled, though he was grinning as Leo shoved his shoulder.

Amy still hadn't moved.

"Great news, Leo," I said, cutting in before the silence grew deafening.

"Awesome," Amy managed to breathe out, her voice barely audible before she went completely quiet.

My chest ached for her. For the rest of the drive, she didn't say a word, staring fixedly out the wide-open window as the city lights blurred past.

When we arrived at the restaurant, I got out first and held out a hand to help her down. She looked surprised, but took it. I wanted to be as supportive as I could without drawing attention to her pain, so I bent my arm, offering it to her. She grabbed hold of it, and a flicker of her usual warmth returned. It was always hard to keep her spirits down for long.

Leo walked up from behind and offered his arm as well.

Amy lightly slapped his arm away with her free hand. "What would your lady love say if you walked in with another woman?" she teased, her tone light and playful.

"Hey, let's not jump to 'love' just yet, guys—" Leo started, but Abhi shoved him out of the way and took Amy’s other arm.

"Mine won't mind," Abhi said smoothly.

Flanked by the two of us, Amy held on tight and walked into the restaurant like she was heading into a battlefield.

The dinner itself passed without incident. We all genuinely liked Natasha. Amy excused herself early, claiming she had a school reunion event to attend. As Abhi and I walked her out to wait for her cab—Abhi fussing over the driver's details and setting a ridiculous curfew for when she needed to text us—I looked at her, wanting nothing more than to wrap her in a hug and tell her that everything was going to be alright.
