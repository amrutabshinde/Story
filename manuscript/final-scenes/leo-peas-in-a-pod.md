The glass door slid shut behind us, cutting off the soft hum of the air conditioner from the living room where Amy and Nick had finally fallen asleep. Abhi, Ash, and I made our way out to the deck, the humid Mumbai night air instantly clinging to my skin. Ash stopped on his way to grab a few cold beers from the kitchen.

The exact moment Ash stepped out onto the deck, he caught Abhi’s eye, and both of them instantly burst out laughing.

I leaned against the deck railing, staring at them blankly. I couldn't understand how they could laugh. I couldn't comprehend how they found anything even remotely funny when Amy had just crushed every single one of my quiet, unspoken dreams by bringing this guy home with her.

"There are two of them," Ash said between wheezing breaths, handing a beer bottle to Abhi. "I swear to God, there are two of them."

"I still can't believe it," Abhi replied, shaking his head and wiping a tear of pure amusement from his eye.

What on earth were these idiots talking about?

"What!" I asked, my voice coming out tighter and sharper than I intended.

"Amy got home a male version of herself," Ash said, and at that, Abhi lost all control and howled with laughter, burying his face in his arms against the terrace wall.

When I still just stood there looking confused and miserable, Ash tried to compose himself to explain further. "Think about it, Leo. He is ridiculously, aggressively over-friendly."

"He actually hugged us all the second he walked through the front door!" Abhi added, chuckling and mimicking Nick’s wide-armed embrace. "Who does that? He didn't even know us!"

"And they both just talk without listening to a single word the other person is saying," Ash said, actual tears of laughter in his eyes now. "It’s just pure, chaotic noise."

"Oh, but did you see the TV?" Abhi chimed in, leaning forward excitedly. "They took one whole hour to watch literally five minutes of *The Lord of the Rings* because they kept pausing it to intensely debate the deep lore of Númenor!"

Ash practically rolled over on the deck floor with laughter at that point, clutching his stomach.

I looked down at the beer bottle in my hands, completely unable to share the joke. What was so funny? I had felt like a heavy, suffocating brick had dropped straight into my stomach from the exact second Nick walked into our apartment. Did my roommates seriously not notice? Were they completely blind to the details that were currently eating me alive?

Nick hadn't just walked in; he had been carrying Amy's heavy luggage right alongside his own without her even asking, handling it like it was his job. And then there was dinner. When the pasta was served, Amy had quietly picked out every single olive from her plate, and Nick had just reached over and eaten them straight out of her dish, completely unbothered, as if it was a routine they had practiced for a lifetime.

Even when it came time to settle down and I handed them pillows to sleep, Nick didn't just take them. He actually pressed his hands into both, testing the fluffiness, and pointedly handed the firmer one to Amy. *Why did he know what kind of pillow Amy uses for her back?* I hated it. I hated how agonizingly familiar they were with one another. It had only been four months since they met in Bangalore, and yet, over the last few weeks, almost every single sentence that came out of Amy’s mouth seemed to have Nick’s name tucked into it somewhere. How could Abhi and Ash not see it?

My mind dragged me right back to the living room, replaying the moment they were setting up their makeshift beds. Nick had looked at the floor mattress and then at the sofa.

*“I’ll take the sofa,”* Nick had offered casually, shaking out a blanket. *“You take the floor so your back doesn’t act up again.”*

Amy had looked up at him from under her eyelashes, *“Ah, so now you sleep up there because you worry about me?”* she had teased.

Nick hadn't missed a beat. He looked down at her, his expression softening into something incredibly steady and intense. *“I always worry about you, Amy.”*

The words themselves had sent a jolt of pure jealousy straight through me, but it was Amy’s reaction that truly broke my heart. She had blushed a deep, brilliant red from the jaw up, biting her lip as a helpless, radiant smile took over her face. She couldn't stop smiling.

I found out later from Abhi that the specific phrasing was just a stupid inside joke, a reference to some *Doctor Who* episode they both loved. But in that exact moment, I was green with jealousy.

I took a long, bitter swig of my beer, the cold liquid doing nothing to soothe the burning ache in my chest. I knew Amy was still healing. I knew her world had completely collapsed when Vic left for Europe, and I had been playing the long, patient game—protecting her, looking after her, waiting for the day she would finally look at me and see someone she could love. I had convinced myself she just wasn't ready for romance yet.

But watching her with Nick tonight made a terrifying truth hit me full force. She *was* capable of letting someone in. She was capable of blushing, laughing, and sharing an effortless intimacy that I had spent months trying to build. She just wasn't doing it with me.

"Hey, Leo, man, you alright?" Abhi’s voice broke through my thoughts. His laughter had finally died down, and he was looking at me with a mild frown.

"Yeah, I'm fine," I lied smoothly, forcing a tight smile as I stared back into the darkened living room through the glass door. On the floor, Amy shifted in her sleep, and even in the dark, I could see Nick instinctively reach out to pull the comforter back over her shoulders.

I turned back to my friends, the weight in my stomach turning cold and permanent. "Just tired. I think I'm going to turn in."
