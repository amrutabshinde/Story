“So we’re picking Amy up on our way?” I asked Abhi, tossing my luggage into the trunk.

We had been planning this trip for months. Usually, Amy stayed over at our flat the night before so we could beat the morning traffic together. But she hadn't come over last night.

"Dude, she’s not coming," Abhi said. Beside him, Ash gave me a look like I’d completely lost my mind.

"What? Why?" I tried—and failed—to mask the sharp drop in my chest.

"She had to fly to Bengaluru for work," Ash said, leaning against the hood. "She’s been talking about it non-stop for a month! How could you not know? I think I even know which hotel they put her in."

I froze. I had thought that was next week. A heavy, bitter weight settled deep in my stomach. Why would Abhi and Ash schedule the trip for a weekend she couldn't make? What was the point if all of us couldn't go together? After everything everyone had been through over the past few months, I had been counting on this road trip to cheer us all up. To cheer her up.

The day felt ruined before it had even begun. Irritated, I took the first shift at the wheel just so I wouldn't have to look at them. Why hadn't anyone mentioned it? I could have convinced everyone to reschedule. Amy needed this break more than any of us—she had been quiet and withdrawn ever since her breakup with Vic. I’d imagined this trip would somehow bring back a piece of the old, lively Amy.

"Are you alright, Leo?" Ash asked from the passenger seat.

I wasn't. And the sheer intensity of my mood was confusing me. If Abhi hadn't been able to make it, I would have missed him, sure. But this wasn't just missing someone. I felt utterly miserable.

"Yeah, why? What's up?" I snapped, snapping out of my thoughts.

"I’ve asked if you want to pull over for a break about five times now," Abhi sighed from the back.

"Are you high?" Ash teased softly.

Eventually, they traded seats so they could sit up front together, leaving me to brood alone in the back. The scenery outside was striking—monsoon rain had turned the hills lush, and temporary waterfalls streamed down the rocky cliffs along the highway.

*Amy would have loved this,* I thought resentfully. Stupid, perfect weather. Why did it have to be so nice when she wasn't here to see it?

Out of petty frustration, I snapped a few photos of the scenery and texted them to her.

*It’s so pretty! I should’ve been there!* she replied almost instantly.

*You should have! Why didn't you come?!*

*I had to take this Bangalore assignment, no other weekend worked :(. But let’s go for a drive when I get back to see the waterfalls? Send me lots of pictures! Have fun!*

She was sickeningly optimistic. But her enthusiasm was infectious, and despite myself, I was already planning a drive back up here the moment she got back.

***

She was away for the entire following week. On Saturday morning, she landed back in Mumbai and took a cab straight from the airport to our flat, carrying a box of specialty donuts she had raved about.

She set the box on the kitchen table and gestured for me to sit down. Abhi brought over mugs of fresh coffee and pulled up a stool beside her.

"Ash, come sit with us," she called out to Ash, who was lounging on the sofa. "There’s something I need to tell you guys."

Ash got up immediately. Abhi and I exchanged a brief, uneasy look.

"So," she began, her hands folded around her coffee mug as we all gathered around the table. "I got offered a new position in Bengaluru."

"What?" Abhi stopped mid-bite.

"Are you taking it?" Ash asked, his mug pausing halfway to his mouth.

"I asked for it," she said quietly.

Silence fell over the kitchen as the three of us stared at her. It had to be a bad joke. Why would she request a transfer to Bangalore? Why would she want to leave Mumbai—leave us?

"Just hear me out," she urged softly. "I’ve been having a really hard time ever since..." She closed her eyes for a brief second, forcing herself past the name. "...ever since Vic. Every coffee shop, every street... everything here just reminds me of him. I can't keep living like this."

"You just need to give yourself more time, Amy," Abhi reasoned gently.

"It’s been four months, Abhi," she pleaded, her voice cracking slightly. "I still have to force myself out of bed every single morning."

It felt like an overreaction. Running away to another city wasn't going to fix anything.

"Amy, stop being impulsive," I blurted out, my frustration spilling over as my palm came down hard on the tabletop.

She reached out, placing her hand gently over mine, looking directly into my eyes. "Leo, please," she begged softly. "I’ve thought about this a lot. I need to try and make things better. I need this."

My thumb instinctively brushed against the side of her hand. Her skin was warm against my cold fingers.

She was leaving us. Leaving me.

The realization landed like a physical blow to the sternum. There was no home without her. There was no life without her. The realization hit with terrifying clarity.I loved her. Panicked by the sudden weight of it, I pulled my hand back from hers with a sharp jerk.

"I need some air," I muttered, scraping my chair back and walking out.

"Leo, come on! Don't be like that!" she called after me, but I didn't stop until I reached the wooden bench in the apartment complex garden.

My throat felt bone-dry. How had I been so completely blind?

A minute later, Ash walked down the path and sat beside me. "Stop being so dramatic," he said quietly.

"Ash, I love her," I choked out, staring straight ahead. "I can't let her go."

"I know," he replied simply.

"No, you don't get it," I insisted, turning to him. "I love her like... I want to spend the rest of my life with her. I don't think I can live without her."

"I know, Leo," Ash repeated, completely unbothered.

I stared at him in disbelief. Either he was being incredibly dense, or he was mocking me.

"I think you've always loved her," Ash said gently. "Ever since she came to stay at our place after hurting her back. You liked her even when you were dating Nat. You just refused to admit it to yourself."

A sudden spike of irritation hit me. If he had seen what I had been too blind to recognize, why on earth hadn't he opened his mouth?

"I have to go back up there and stop her," I said, starting to push myself off the bench.

"You have to let her go, Leo," Ash said, catching my arm gently. "She needs space."

"If I let her go, I will lose her!"

"It just means we'll have to bring her back later," Ash reasoned. "She’s still recovering from Vic, Leo. And I don't even know if she still has that crush on you."

I froze. "What?"

"She had a massive crush on you , I think, ever since we met." Ash said.

Now I genuinely wanted to strangle him. What was the actual point of having Ash as a roommate if he kept life-altering information to himself? "Why the hell didn't you say anything back then?!" I yelled, shoving his hand off my arm.

"Oh, so now this is my fault?!" Ash shot back, his quiet demeanor snapping into irritation. "You went and started dating Nat! And then she started seeing Vic! What was I supposed to do!"

I dragged both hands through my hair, clutching at the roots as my head throbbed.

"I'm going back inside," Ash said, standing up and dusting off his jeans. "Get your head together, come upstairs, and try acting like a supportive friend."

He turned and walked back toward the building entrance. I stayed in the garden for a few more minutes, kicking loose gravel across the path until my toes throbbed inside my shoes.

When I finally pulled myself together and walked back up to the flat...
