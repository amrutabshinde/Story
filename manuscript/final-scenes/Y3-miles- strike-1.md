I had been trying not to think about Nick ever since Amy’s frantic call woke me around midnight yesterday. She was completely hysterical. She and Nick had been out for dinner when he suddenly fell violently ill, forcing her to rush him straight to the hospital emergency room.

I tried not to think about him—I couldn't afford to panic. I had to make it from Mumbai to Bangalore without falling apart. To stay grounded, I forced myself to process Amy’s panicked rambling like it was a crime report I was working on -  taking down facts for an investigation: They had dinner at a seafood restaurant. Nick started feeling flushed and unwell, and by the time Amy got him to the hospital, he was vomiting uncontrollably. The doctors suspected acute food poisoning from the fish.

But something about the situation didn't sit right with me.

On my way to the Mumbai airport, I made a flurry of quick calls. On the flight to Bangalore, I dug into the background details: the restaurant had an flawless track record with zero health violations or reports of negligence. Furthermore, no other patrons had reported food poisoning that night.

Nick didn't just work anywhere; he worked for a private research facility handling classified projects for the Ministry of Defence. It was all strictly off-the-record. Could someone have targeted him? Was this an attempt on his life?

My throat tightened, and for a terrifying second, I couldn't catch my breath. Focus. Focus, I ordered myself. I threw all my energy into researching my foul-play theory, using the investigation as a mental shield so I wouldn't have to face the paralyzing horror of a world where I lost him.

Even after landing in Bangalore and reaching the hospital, I took my time before stepping into his room. I needed a moment to gather my composure. I spoke with Amy first, convincing her to go home and get some sleep, and then conferred with his attending doctor. The doctor reassured me that Nick had arrived just in time before any permanent organ damage occurred, though his system was severely compromised.

Stepping into the room, my protective armor shattered.

Watching him sleep—surrounded by the rhythmic hum of monitors, IV lines taped to his skin, and the pale hospital sheets—felt completely wrong. Nick was usually so full of life, so irrepressible. Part of me kept waiting for him to pop up from the mattress and yell "Got you!" as if this was all part of some twisted, elaborate joke he was playing on me.

I sat down in the chair Amy had been praying in before I sent her home, dragging it right against the side of the bed. I reached out and took his hand, holding tight to feel the solid warmth of his skin. I anchored myself to the steady rise and fall of his chest and the sharp blips on the heart monitor.

He's fine, I assured myself repeatedly. He's going to be fine. Because I honestly didn't know what I would do if he wasn't.

Sometime in the wee hours of the morning, Nick stirred. His eyelids fluttered open, and I immediately leaned closer. He blinked against the dim hospital light, his unfocused gaze wandering until it settled on me. A weak, familiar smile touched his lips.

"Hi," he croaked, his voice raw and dry.

Hearing him speak felt like the first real breath I had taken since midnight. "Hi," I whispered, rushing closer to his pillow.

"What happened?" he asked, a faint frown of confusion creasing his brow. "I had a stomach ache after dinner with Amy... Amy... where is she? Is she alright?"

"She's fine," I reassured him softly. "I sent her home to get some sleep. You had food poisoning."

"Damn it... sushi," he declared with mock annoyance.

"How are you feeling?"

"I've been better," he admitted quietly. "Could you get me some water, please?"

I poured a glass and held it for him as he took slow, cautious sips. Every swallow seemed to hurt his raw throat, though he tried not to complain. When he cleared his throat, a sharp cough escaped, making him wince as pain shot through his chest and stomach. He didn't say a word about it, but I could see the strain etched across his pale face.

"You came all the way from Mumbai?" he asked, looking up at me.

"Amy called," I said, trying to keep my tone level. "She didn't know who else to reach out to..."

A faint smirk played on his lips. "Did you cry for me?"

"Shut up, Nick."

"Oh, so sweet... you were worried I was dying," he teased softly.

Something inside me snapped. I was in no mood for his lighthearted deflective humor.

"Is this a joke to you, Nick?" my voice cracked, anger spilling out to cover the sheer terror I’d been suppressing. "Do you have any idea what I've been through over the last few hours? You could have had permanent damage. You could have—" I stopped. Started again, quieter. "I don't know how to deal with it. If something had happened to you I don't know what I would—"

His smirk faded completely. "Miles..." he murmured, his tone turning impossibly soft.

Exposed and suddenly flustered by my raw admission, I lashed out defensively. "Don't 'Miles' me," I snapped, more furious at my own vulnerability than at him. "Just... don't eat stuff that doesn't suit your precious little tummy again."

"Okay. I won't," he said obediently, his voice remarkably gentle.

Before he could say anything more, another violent coughing fit seized him. His breath caught, and his whole body tensed in pain. His throat was raw, his lungs aching with every shuddering breath. He clutched his chest and stomach tightly, curling inward until the fit finally subsided, his eyes watering from the sheer physical effort.

Seeing him in so much pain broke whatever was left of my composure. Tears stung my eyes, spilling hot down my cheeks. I tried to wipe them away, but they kept coming. The terrifying truth echoed through my head with absolute clarity: I can't lose him. I won't let anyone hurt him.

I gripped his hand tighter. Nick looked up, his watery eyes meeting mine as he noticed my tears. Dropping all teasing pretenses, he offered a quiet, brave smile.

"I'm fine," he whispered softly.

"I hate you," I sobbed, wiping my face with the back of my hand.

Nick's fingers tightened reassuringly around mine. "I know."
